# Moiré Patterns in Computer Graphics: A Literature Review

## TL;DR
- The computer-graphics literature on moiré is dominated by a single center of gravity — Roger D. Hersch's Peripheral Systems Lab (LSP) at EPFL, whose SIGGRAPH/TOG papers "Band Moiré Images" (2004) and "Beating Shapes Relying on Moiré Level Lines" (2014), building on Isaac Amidror's foundational Fourier theory, established moiré *generation* as a rigorous design problem for hidden imagery, document security, and animated art.
- Beyond EPFL, the field spans a handful of clearly identifiable threads: target-driven moiré synthesis (Tsai & Chuang, ICCV 2013), refractive/lenslet hidden-image displays (Papas et al., "The Magic Lens," SIGGRAPH Asia 2012), moiré-free vs. deliberately-exploited halftoning, microlens "moiré magnifier" imaging, and a recent burst of interactive/real-time moiré for tracking and tangible interfaces (MoiréBoard, MoiréTag, MoiréWidgets, FabObscura).
- The clearest open gaps are a lack of general, artist-facing *interactive authoring tools* for moiré, little work on moiré on arbitrary curved/3D-printed surfaces in top graphics venues, and almost no learning-based *generative* moiré-art work (deep learning has focused overwhelmingly on the inverse problem, demoiréing).

## Key Findings
- **Foundational theory:** Isaac Amidror's *The Theory of the Moiré Phenomenon* (Kluwer 2000; 2nd ed. Springer, Vol. I Periodic Layers 2009, Vol. II Aperiodic Layers 2007) is the Fourier-based mathematical backbone cited by essentially every graphics moiré paper. Earlier touchstones are Oster & Nishijima's *Scientific American* article "Moiré Patterns" (1963) and Oster, Wasserman & Zwerling, "Theoretical Interpretation of Moiré Patterns," JOSA (1964).
- **Band moiré (the canonical graphics generation method):** Hersch & Chosson, "Band Moiré Images," SIGGRAPH 2004 / ACM TOG 23(3):239–247, gives a general model predicting the geometry (orientation, size, displacement) of a moiré as a linear/nonlinear transform of a base band layer sampled by a revealing line grating. The abstract states directly: "By offering a new means of artistic expression, band moiré images can be used to create new designs and to synthesize visually appealing applications."
- **Level-line / beating-shape moiré:** Chosson & Hersch, "Beating Shapes Relying on Moiré Level Lines," ACM TOG 34(1), Article 9, 2014, converts bilevel shapes into elevation profiles embedded as small shifts in a base grating, whose level lines are revealed as moiré that "beats" upon displacement.
- **Target-driven synthesis:** Tsai & Chuang, "Target-Driven Moiré Pattern Synthesis by Phase Modulation," ICCV 2013, generate two grating images whose superposition reproduces an arbitrary target image via the (1,−1)-moiré component phase constraint. The method "enables the creation of moire art and allows visual decoding without computers by obtaining the phase modulation constraint on the phase shifts between the two grating images."
- **Hidden imagery / optical steganography:** Papas et al., "The Magic Lens: Refractive Steganography," ACM TOG (SIGGRAPH Asia) 31(6):186, 2012, and Pjanic & Hersch, "Color Imaging and Pattern Hiding on a Metallic Substrate," ACM TOG (SIGGRAPH) 34(4):130, 2015, both exploit related superposition/hiding principles for aesthetic hidden-image reveal.
- **Interactive/real-time moiré:** A recent wave uses moiré motion-amplification for sensing — MoiréBoard (UIST 2021), MoiréTag (SIGGRAPH 2023), and MoiréWidgets (CHI 2024) — plus FabObscura (UIST 2025) for barrier-grid animation authoring.

## Details

### 1. Foundational theory of moiré used in graphics
The mathematical foundation for graphics work is **Isaac Amidror's *The Theory of the Moiré Phenomenon*** (Kluwer Academic, 2000; 2nd revised ed., Springer — Volume I: Periodic Layers, 2009, and Volume II: Aperiodic Layers, 2007), part of the Computational Imaging and Vision series (Vol. 38). It provides a general, application-independent, Fourier-based account of what happens spectrally and spatially when repetitive layers are superposed. Amidror is a retired EPFL professor and the theory underpins essentially every EPFL graphics moiré paper. A key companion journal paper is **Amidror & Hersch, "Fourier-based analysis and synthesis of moirés in the superposition of geometrically transformed periodic structures," JOSA A 15:1100–1113 (1998)**, and Amidror's "A generalized Fourier-based method for the analysis of 2D moiré envelope-forms in screen superpositions," J. Mod. Opt. 41(9):1837–1862 (1994).

Historically important precursors (used as brief background in graphics papers, not as graphics work themselves) include **A. Righi (1887)**, the earliest systematic study; **Gerald Oster & Yasunori Nishijima, "Moiré Patterns," Scientific American 208(5):54–63 (1963)**; **Oster, Wasserman & Zwerling, "Theoretical Interpretation of Moiré Patterns," JOSA 54(2):169–175 (1964)**; and **Gerald Oster, "Optical Art," Applied Optics 4(11):1359–1369 (1965)**, which connects moiré directly to the Op-Art movement (Bridget Riley, Victor Vasarely). D. Tollenaar's 1945 monograph on moiré in halftone printing is the historical root of the printing thread.

### 2. Moiré synthesis / generation techniques (the core graphics contribution)

**Band moiré images — Hersch & Chosson, SIGGRAPH 2004, ACM TOG 23(3):239–247.** This is the seminal graphics moiré-generation paper. Treating moiré formation as a *line-sampling* process, the authors derive the linear transformation between a "base layer" (a band grating whose bands contain a one-dimensionally compressed image of shapes/letters) and the resulting moiré image, predicting its orientation, size and displacement direction as the revealing layer is moved. By applying freely chosen nonlinear geometric transformations to base and revealing layers, they synthesize moiré components that move up/down at different speeds and orientations, and derive layer transformations that yield periodic moirés even from curved layers. The paper explicitly positions band moiré as "a new means of artistic expression" for "visually appealing applications" as well as security, drawing a clear line from the informal artistic work of Joe Huck ("Mastering Moirés," 2003) to a general predictive framework.

**Beating shapes / level-line moiré — Chosson & Hersch, ACM TOG 34(1), Article 9, 2014** (also presented in the SIGGRAPH 2015 Technical Papers program). The method obtains a recognizable shape as the superposition moiré of two line gratings: the moiré lines sit between the shape foreground/background centers and boundaries. Bilevel shapes are converted into elevation profiles embedded as small local shifts within the base grating; the elevation profile's *level lines* are revealed as moiré. Upon relative displacement the moiré lines move, giving the impression of "beating" shapes. This distinguishes level-line moiré from band moiré: the revealed lines look like offset contour lines rather than enlarged base shapes. Sylvain Chosson's EPFL PhD thesis "Synthèse d'images moiré" (EPFL Thesis 3434, 2006) is the underlying dissertation.

**Target-driven moiré by phase modulation — Tsai & Chuang (National Taiwan University), ICCV 2013, pp. 1912–1919.** Given a target image, the method computes two grating images so that the moiré of their superposition resembles the target. It is grounded in the "fundamental moiré theorem," focusing on the visually dominant (1,−1)-moiré component to derive a phase-modulation constraint on the phase shifts between the gratings. A smoothness term spreads information between the two gratings (improving appearance and hiding capability), and an "appearance phase function" injects irregular structure. Gratings can be printed on transparencies and decoded optically by overlay; the method "enables the creation of moire art and allows visual decoding without computers." This is the most prominent non-EPFL generation method and the closest to a general image-to-moiré solver.

**Variational approaches — Lebanon & Bruckstein (Technion).** "Variational approach to moiré pattern synthesis," JOSA A 18(6):1371–1382 (2001), with companion papers "Designing Moiré Patterns" (EMMCVPR 2001, LNCS 2134) and "On Designing Moiré Patterns" (2002). They formalize moiré synthesis as choosing among the (non-unique) images that produce a desired superposition, introducing performance criteria based on human-visual-system visibility and digitization, then solving via a variational / height-from-gradient formulation. This is the principled optimization counterpart to Hersch's constructive geometry.

### 3. Moiré for hidden imagery, document security and anti-counterfeiting (the largest applied thread)

This is where the EPFL group's influence is deepest, spanning graphics papers and a large patent portfolio. The security appeal is that moiré is extremely sensitive to micrometer-scale registration/print errors, so it magnifies counterfeiting inaccuracies into visible distortions.

- **2D moiré intensity profiles:** Amidror & Hersch's earliest security method (US Pat. 6,249,588, filed 1995; continuation 5,995,638; and 6,819,775) superposes a specially designed 2D base dot-screen with a revealing dot/pinhole/microlens array to produce a moiré intensity profile of chosen shape whose size/orientation vary as layers move.
- **Moiré cryptography / correlated random layers:** Amidror, "Authentication with built-in encryption by using moiré intensity profiles between random layers" (US Pat. 7,058,202), and Amidror & Hersch (US Pat. 8,351,087) use correlated random ("s-random") dot-screens (Glass-pattern-like) so a single moiré shape appears only when a base and its correlated revealing layer superpose — effectively a visual one-time-pad.
- **1D band moiré security:** the SIGGRAPH 2004 band-moiré method productized via US Pat. 7,751,608 and 7,710,551.
- **Level-line shape moiré security:** Chosson & Hersch, "Authentication of secure items by shape level lines" (US Pat. 7,305,105).
- **Survey:** Amidror, Chosson & Hersch, "Moiré methods for the protection of documents and products: A short survey," J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 77:012001 (2007).
- **Microfabricated 1D moiré:** Cadarso, Chosson, Sidler, Hersch & Brugger, "High-resolution 1D moirés as counterfeit security features," *Light: Science & Applications* 2 (2013), demonstrating micrometer-scale 1D moiré unreproducible by standard printing.

Related hidden-imagery work from other groups:
- **Papas, Houit, Nowrouzezahrai, Gross & Jarosz, "The Magic Lens: Refractive Steganography," ACM TOG (SIGGRAPH Asia) 31(6):186, 2012** (Disney Research Zürich / ETH). A passive display: a refractive lenslet array warps an unstructured source image into a meaningful hidden target, solving an inverse light-transport problem for the lens geometry; different lens rotations/angles reveal different hidden images. Conceptually adjacent to moiré magnification (superposition-based reveal) though it is refraction-based. Cited references situate it among "Emerging Images" (Mitra et al. 2009), "Shadow Art" (Mitra & Pauly 2009), and Naor & Shamir's visual cryptography.
- **Pjanic & Hersch, "Color Imaging and Pattern Hiding on a Metallic Substrate," ACM TOG (SIGGRAPH) 34(4):130, 2015.** Prints on a metallic substrate that hide text/graphics/grayscale images in one viewing mode (specular vs. non-specular) and reveal them in the other by trading off white-diffuse ink vs. CMY ink; images can alternate between two independent pictures as the print is tilted.
- **Kita & Miyata, "Magic Sheets: Visual Cryptography with Common Shares," *Computational Visual Media* 4(2):185–195, 2018.** A transparency-superposition visual-cryptography scheme that cites and builds conceptually on beating-shape/level-line moiré, the Magic Lens, and Pjanic & Hersch; a "magic sheet" (common share) decrypts multiple secret images.

### 4. Moiré with optical devices for imagery (moiré magnifiers, lenslets, lenticular)

The "moiré magnifier" — an array of micro-images viewed through a slightly mismatched microlens array producing a magnified, floating synthetic image — originates with **Hutley, Hunt, Stevens & Savander, "The Moiré Magnifier," Pure and Applied Optics 3:133–142 (1994)** and **Kamal, Völkel & Alda, "Properties of Moiré Magnifiers," Optical Engineering 37(11):3007–3014 (1998)**. Though optics venues, these are the foundation for graphics/fabrication imaging (and for banknote security films such as Steenblik et al.'s micro-optic systems, US Pat. 7,333,268).

The EPFL group extended this to lenslet-based moiré as an imaging medium: **Walger, Besson, Flauraud, Hersch & Brugger, "1D moiré shapes by superposed layers of micro-lenses," Optics Express 27(26):37419–37434 (2019)** and **"Level-line moirés by superposition of cylindrical microlens gratings," JOSA A 37(2):209–218 (2020)**, and **Saberpour, Hersch et al., "Fabrication of moiré on curved surfaces," Optics Express 28(13):19413–19427 (2020)**. The consolidating patent, Hersch, Walger, Flauraud, Brugger & Besson, "Synthesis of superposition shape images by light interacting with layers of lenslets" (US Pat. 10,286,716), explicitly lists applications spanning "protection of documents… eye-catching advertisements as well as the decoration of buildings and exhibitions." These are optics-venue papers but treat moiré strictly as a designed imaging/aesthetic target, so are in scope as the physical-realization arm of the graphics work. Earlier related optics precursors include Mikami's moiré-lens imaging papers (1975).

### 5. Interactive tools and real-time moiré

A recent cluster uses the moiré effect's motion amplification for sensing and interaction — the most "interactive" moiré graphics/HCI work to date:
- **MoiréBoard — Chang Xiao & Changxi Zheng (Columbia), UIST 2021, pp. 881–893.** A 3-DOF camera position-tracking method leveraging "a seemingly irrelevant visual phenomenon, the moiré effect"; a passive, 3D-printable marker that "requires no power nor camera calibration" and tracks devices "at high accuracy, comparable to the state-of-the-art commercial VR tracking systems."
- **MoiréTag — Simeng Qiu, Hadi Amata & Wolfgang Heidrich (KAUST), SIGGRAPH 2023 Conference Proceedings, Article 26.** A passive marker that encodes angular information into a moiré pattern printed on two sides of a glass wafer; angular shifts appear as a phase shift of the lowest moiré frequency, fused across frequencies by an MLP, extended via a chirp model to full 6D tracking plus camera-intrinsics estimation. The authors report that "the proposed non-contact object tracking framework is computationally efficient and the average angular accuracy of 0.17° outperforms the state-of-the-arts."
- **MoiréWidgets — Campos Zamora, Dogan, Siu, Koh & Chang Xiao, CHI 2024 (DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642734).** Passive, battery-free tangible widgets (buttons, sliders, dials) built from 3D-printed mechanisms with fringe patterns on a paper layer and a plastic transparency; the moiré amplifies subtle movements and an image-processing pipeline reads input from the fringe phase shift. The paper reports the method "outperforms standard fiducial markers and maintains sub-millimeter accuracy at 100 cm distance and wide viewing angles."
- **FabObscura — Sethapakdi, Perroni-Scharf, Li, Li, Solomon, Satyanarayan & Mueller (MIT CSAIL), UIST 2025, Article 109.** A computational design/fabrication system for interactive barrier-grid ("picket-fence") animations, parameterizing occlusion patterns as mathematical functions to support curved/radial/spiral/multi-directional barriers and sliding, rotational and view-dependent interaction. (Barrier-grid animation is an occlusion/parallax technique adjacent to — but distinct from — true two-grating interference moiré; included with that caveat. It explicitly cites MoiréWidgets as related work.)

For completeness in the sensing/security-adjacent space (outside the graphics/HCI core, and outside the review's creative-generation focus), note also camera-to-screen moiré sensing/watermarking such as MoiréVision (MobiCom 2024), MoiréPose (MobiCom 2022), and screen-photo tracing/attacks (mID, USENIX Security 2021; Moiré Attack, NeurIPS 2021). These are applied-security/mobile works, mentioned only as a related thread.

### 6. Moiré in halftoning and printing (avoiding — and exploiting — moiré)

Color halftoning is the domain where moiré is classically an *artifact to suppress*: superposing periodic clustered-dot screens for CMYK beats together into visible moiré, traditionally avoided by rotating identical square screens to angles maximally separated (e.g., 15°/45°/75°). Graphics/imaging work on principled moiré-free design includes non-orthogonal single-cell clustered-dot screens and lattice-based / uniform-rosette N-color moiré-free screening (largely from the Xerox / Purdue electronic-imaging community — e.g., work associated with Wang, Allebach, Sharma and colleagues; US Pat. 6,798,539 on non-orthogonal cluster screens; Delabastita's rational-tangent screening, US Pat. 5,155,599). These appear in electronic-imaging venues more than SIGGRAPH but are directly relevant as the "avoid moiré" pole.

Deliberate *exploitation* of the halftoning layer for aesthetics is the EPFL "artistic screening" line — the direct conceptual ancestor of the band-moiré work:
- **Ostromoukhov & Hersch, "Artistic Screening," SIGGRAPH 1995, pp. 219–228.** Freely-designed artistic screen elements (e.g., calligraphic shapes) act as halftone dots that grow through predefined contours, turning the screening layer into a carrier of designed micro-imagery; anti-counterfeiting features of artistic screening were developed in Ostromoukhov, Rudaz, Amidror, Emmel & Hersch, Proc. SPIE 2951:126–133 (1996).
- **Ostromoukhov & Hersch, "Multi-Color and Artistic Dithering," SIGGRAPH 1999, pp. 425–432.** Multi-color dithering placing non-standard inks side-by-side, with applications to advertisements, posters, and security printing, and to special (fluorescent/metallic) inks.
- **Hersch, Donzé & Chosson, "Color images visible under UV light," SIGGRAPH 2007, ACM TOG 26(3), Article 75.** Fluorescent-ink imaging for document security.

### 7. Moiré artifacts in rendering and displays

In rendering, moiré appears as a *spatial-aliasing artifact* when high-frequency texture/geometry is undersampled (Nyquist violation), classically mitigated by supersampling and prefiltering (mipmapping, cone/ray footprint filtering) — and more recently in neural rendering (Mip-NeRF and successors; anti-aliased 3D Gaussian splatting). The dominant *learning-based* moiré thread is the inverse problem, **demoiréing** (removing camera-capture-of-screen moiré), which is out of the review's main scope but forms a large related literature (e.g., wavelet/frequency-plus-spatial networks; UniDemoiré 2025 for universal demoiréing with data generation). A handful of recent works instead *generate* moiré as data augmentation or for watermarking (e.g., moiré-pattern-generation-based image steganography, J. Information Security and Applications, 2024). Deliberate creative exploitation of rendering/display moiré in top graphics venues is essentially absent — a notable gap.

### 8. Moiré as an artistic/aesthetic medium (generative & kinetic art)

Beyond peer-reviewed CS venues, moiré has a rich art tradition documented in Leonardo and adjacent outlets: e.g., **James F. Watkins, "Kinetic Art: Producing Unusual Moiré Effects by Means of Specially Prepared Drawings," Leonardo 15(2):133–136 (1982)**, describing illuminated wall pieces of continuously morphing moiré fields. Oster's "Optical Art" (1965) ties moiré to Op-Art (Bridget Riley, Victor Vasarely). Carsten Nicolai's *Moiré Index* (Gestalten, 2010) is a design-oriented catalog, and there is a small architecture/façade literature on layered "visual interference." These are secondary context for the graphics contributions rather than technical graphics papers, but they document the aesthetic tradition that the Hersch/Tsai lines formalize.

### Chronological arc and influential groups
- **1887–1969:** Physics/optics groundwork (Righi; Oster & Nishijima; Oster/Wasserman/Zwerling) and the halftone-printing observation (Tollenaar 1945); Oster's "Optical Art" (1965) frames the aesthetic.
- **1994–2000:** Moiré-magnifier optics (Hutley et al. 1994; Kamal et al. 1998) and Amidror's unifying Fourier theory (book, 2000; JOSA A 1998).
- **1995–1999:** EPFL "artistic screening" and "multi-color dithering" turn the halftone screen into an expressive/secure carrier.
- **2001:** Lebanon & Bruckstein's variational synthesis framework.
- **2004:** Hersch & Chosson "Band Moiré Images" (SIGGRAPH) — the field's landmark generation paper.
- **2007–2014:** EPFL security/aesthetic extensions (UV-visible color images 2007; level-line/beating shapes TOG 2014).
- **2012–2015:** Non-EPFL hidden-image displays and printing (Magic Lens 2012; Tsai & Chuang target-driven synthesis 2013; Pjanic & Hersch metallic-substrate hiding 2015).
- **2019–2020:** EPFL lenslet-array and curved-surface moiré fabrication.
- **2021–2025:** Interactive/real-time moiré for sensing and fabrication (MoiréBoard, MoiréTag, MoiréWidgets, FabObscura).

The clearly dominant group is **Roger D. Hersch's lab at EPFL** (with co-authors Sylvain Chosson, Isaac Amidror, Victor Ostromoukhov, Petar Pjanic, Thomas Walger, and collaborator Juergen Brugger). Secondary hubs are **Yung-Yu Chuang (NTU)** for target-driven synthesis, **Wojciech Jarosz / Markus Gross (Disney Research/ETH)** for refractive hidden images, **Alfred Bruckstein (Technion)** for variational theory, and a newer **HCI/graphics cluster** (Changxi Zheng & Chang Xiao at Columbia/BU; Wolfgang Heidrich at KAUST; Stefanie Mueller at MIT) for interactive moiré.

## Recommendations
- **Start here (must-read core):** Amidror's *Theory of the Moiré Phenomenon* (theory); Hersch & Chosson SIGGRAPH 2004 (band moiré); Chosson & Hersch TOG 2014 (level-line/beating shapes); Tsai & Chuang ICCV 2013 (target-driven synthesis); Papas et al. SIGGRAPH Asia 2012 (Magic Lens). These five cover the theoretical spine plus the three principal generation paradigms (band, level-line, phase-modulation) and the leading hidden-image display.
- **If your interest is generation/authoring:** treat Hersch's constructive geometry and Lebanon & Bruckstein's variational optimization as complementary; a clear opportunity is a modern, GPU-interactive or differentiable authoring tool combining them (none exists in top venues). Benchmark against Tsai & Chuang for arbitrary-target fidelity. A threshold worth setting: if you can express the moiré as a phase-modulation constraint (Tsai/Chuang) you get arbitrary raster targets; if you need clean animated line/shape motion, use the level-line/band formulation.
- **If your interest is fabrication/displays:** follow the EPFL lenslet line (Walger et al. 2019–2020; curved-surface moiré 2020) and the Magic Lens; the decision threshold is whether you need cheap planar transparency overlays (band/level-line moiré) vs. microlens arrays (floating/animated magnifier effects, higher fabrication cost but stronger depth/animation).
- **If your interest is interaction:** MoiréBoard, MoiréTag and MoiréWidgets are the state of the art for moiré-as-sensor; the open direction is closing the loop into *authoring* (interactive design of the aesthetic moiré itself, not just reading it). FabObscura is the closest existing authoring tool but targets barrier-grid rather than interference moiré.
- **Watch for:** heavy patent-vs-paper duplication in the EPFL corpus (many results appear first or only as patents, e.g., "Synthesis of moving and beating moiré shapes," "Synthesis of curved surface moiré"); and be careful to scope out the very large demoiréing and optics-metrology literatures, which dominate keyword searches but fall outside creative generation.

## Caveats
- **Venue skew:** The strongest "generation + aesthetics" results cluster in a small number of groups (overwhelmingly Hersch/EPFL). Much of the EPFL output lives in patents and optics journals (Optics Express, JOSA A) rather than SIGGRAPH/TOG; I have included these where they treat moiré as a design/imaging target, per the review's scope, but flag them as not-core-graphics venues.
- **Barrier-grid vs. true moiré:** FabObscura and lenticular/parallax-barrier animation are occlusion/parallax techniques, related to but not identical with two-grating interference moiré; treat them as adjacent. Likewise the Magic Lens is refraction-based rather than interference-based.
- **Reported metrics** (e.g., MoiréTag's 0.17° angular accuracy; MoiréWidgets' sub-millimeter accuracy at 100 cm; MoiréBoard's "comparable to commercial VR") are the authors' own reported results, not independently verified. MoiréWidgets was published at CHI 2024 (reported acceptance rate ~26.3%).
- **Minor bibliographic note:** the ACM Digital Library lists "Band Moiré Images" as ACM TOG 23(3):239–247; several secondary sources cite 239–248. Cite the ACM DL pagination (239–247).
- **Completeness:** Moiré is a heavily overloaded term; the demoiréing (removal) and physics/metrology literatures are vast and were deliberately excluded except as brief related threads. Additional generative-art pieces in Bridges/NPAR/Leonardo may exist beyond those cited here.
