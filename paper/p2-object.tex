\section{The object}
\label{sec:object}

Beating has been studied on sinusoids for two centuries, and on sinusoids
there is nothing to study. The product identity
$\cos\omega_1t+\cos\omega_2t=2\cos\tfrac{\omega_1+\omega_2}2t\,\cos\tfrac{\omega_1-\omega_2}2t$
collapses the whole phenomenon into two factors so tidy that no question is
left: the envelope is a cosine, the carrier is a cosine, and the observer's
only job is to fail to resolve one of them. A drawing is not a sinusoid. Two
families of strokes carry a full ladder of harmonics each, and what emerges
when they are superposed is a rich object --- a saturating tent rather than a
cosine, a lattice of stations, exact nulls, quantized defects, a locking
staircase --- that the identity hid. This paper is a theory of that object.
It rests on one formal move, which is to stop describing a pattern by its
frequencies and describe it by what it counts.

\begin{definition}[Counting map, ink potential, drawing]
\label{def:drawing}
A stack of $K$ scalar-index layers is a map
\[
  \Phi:\R^2\to\tor^K,\qquad \Phi(p)=\big(\idx_1(p),\dots,\idx_K(p)\big)\bmod 1,
\]
whose $i$-th coordinate is the fractional member index of layer $i$ at $p$,
together with an ink potential $I:\tor^K\to[0,1]$, the paint-order composite
of the per-coordinate stroke profiles. The drawing is $D=I\circ\Phi$.
\end{definition}

A layer's index counts its members: the $n$-th line, the $n$-th ring, the
$n$-th ray, with the count interpolated between them. Everything geometric
about a stack is in $\Phi$ --- pitches, centres, walks, twists, fields ---
and everything about ink is in $I$, a fixed function on a torus that knows
nothing of the plane. The factorisation is the whole tool: a renderer
evaluates $\Phi$ (by a closed form or a certified search) and looks $I$ up.

\begin{definition}[Characters, local frequency, slowness]
\label{def:character}
A character is $k\in\Z^K=H^1(\tor^K;\Z)$, acting as $\chi_k(x)=e^{2\pi i\,k\cdot x}$.
Its pullback $k\cdot\Phi$ has local frequency $J_p^{\top}k=\nabla(k\cdot\Phi)(p)$
with $J_p=D\Phi(p)$, and $k$ is \emph{slow} at $p$ when $|J_p^{\top}k|$ is small
against the carriers $|J_p^{\top}e_i|$. A moir\'e is the pullback of a slow
character.
\end{definition}

The integers $k$ are the moir\'e orders of the classical theory, and the
definition says where they live: not in a Fourier plane, which requires a
periodic drawing to exist, but in the integer cohomology of the target torus,
which every stack has, periodic or not, and pointwise. The difference
character $(1,-1)$ is the classical moir\'e of two gratings; $(2,-1)$ is the
station of a $2{:}1$ pair; $(1,1,-2)$ is a beat between beats of three ring
families. Which of them is visible at a point is the subject of Law~I.

\begin{remark}[Fourier is bookkeeping]
For a product potential $I=\prod_i g_i(x_i)$, $\hat I(k)=\prod_i\hat g_i(k_i)$,
and a stroke's harmonics decay like $|\hat g(n)|\sim 1/n$. The amplitude weight
$|k_1k_2\cdots|$ of \S\ref{sec:selection} is this decay and nothing else; no
other Fourier statement is used.
\end{remark}

Three finite questions can be asked of $\Phi$, and the paper's three laws are
their answers. Which characters are slow, and how are they priced --- an
arithmetic question, answered by lattice reduction and continued fractions
(\S\ref{sec:selection}). Which characters survive an average --- an analytic
question, answered by a conditional expectation on a subtorus, exact for the
tool's sweep and universal for every observer that pools before it decides
(\S\ref{sec:averaging}). And whether the count exists as a function at all ---
a topological question, answered by the branch structure of an implicit
function, with a fold law that is computed and a defect charge that is
quantized (\S\ref{sec:integrability}). Every claim with a number is measured
by a script and gathered in \S\ref{sec:predictions}.

\paragraph{Where this sits.}
The indicial method of the moir\'e literature draws the $(1,-1)$ fringes of
two families as the level sets of an index difference, and its author's own
verdict --- that the method ignores intensity profiles --- names exactly what
the amplitude weight restores. Fourier moir\'e theory is global and needs a
periodic drawing; the torus is local and needs none. De Bruijn's index
function for pentagrids and multigrids is a counting map by another name,
dualised into tilings and never asked about visibility. The hull torus of
incommensurate physics is the same torus, asked spectral questions. This paper
sits at their junction, and the visibility theory --- selection, averaging,
integrability, and the observer theorem --- is what it adds.
