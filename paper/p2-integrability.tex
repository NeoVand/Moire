\section{Law III: integrability, the topology of the count}
\label{sec:integrability}

The first two laws take the counting map as given. This one asks whether it
exists. A family is a set of curves with an index convention; the fractional
index at a point is the real $\nu$ such that the point lies on the
interpolated member $\nu$, and that is an implicit function. Whether it is a
function at all, and of what, is the whole taxonomy of patterns.

\begin{theorem}[Trichotomy]
\label{thm:trichotomy}
Let $F(p,\nu)=0$ be the incidence of a family (the point $p$ lies on the member
of continuous index $\nu$). The count $\nu(p)$ exists in exactly three ways:
as a global lift (the exact rung), as a lift on a cover with monodromy
$\pi_1\to\Z$ (the winding rung, whose defects carry quantized charge), or only
as a correspondence branched along the discriminant $\partial_\nu F=0$ (the
fold rung). A field moves $\Phi$ within its homotopy class or changes its
monodromy by a quantized amount, and never creates a discriminant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Where $\partial_\nu F\ne0$ the implicit function theorem gives $\nu$ locally
as a smooth function of $p$, and the local branches glue into a function on
the domain exactly when their continuation around every loop returns to the
same value; the obstruction is a homomorphism from the fundamental group of
the domain to the deck group of the index, which is $\Z$, and its image is
the set of charges. Where $\partial_\nu F=0$ the incidence is not a graph
over $p$: two members meet the point at the same index to first order, the
branches join, and no cover restores a function. A field composes $F$ with a
shift of $\nu$ by a function of $p$, which leaves $\partial_\nu F$ unchanged.
\end{proof}

The three rungs are decided in closed form for the families a drawing tool
ships, because those families are of two kinds.

\begin{proposition}[Carrier classification]
\label{prop:carriers}
Every closed-form family is the orbit of a seed curve under one of the four
one-parameter similarity flows of the plane (translation, rotation, dilation,
loxodrome) or a propagating front. An orbit family folds only where the flow
is tangent to the seed, so transverse seeds never fold; it winds exactly when
the flow has a rotational part. The alphabet is closed: the two carriers the
classification names that no catalogue shipped are the dilation clock
(geometric rings) and the loxodromic clock (the log spiral).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For an orbit family, member $\nu$ is $\Psi_\nu(\gamma)$ with $\Psi$ the flow,
so the incidence is $F(p,\nu)=G(\Psi_{-\nu}p)$ for a defining function $G$
of the seed, and $\partial_\nu F=-\nabla G\cdot\xi(\Psi_{-\nu}p)$ with $\xi$
the flow's generator: it vanishes exactly where the flow is tangent to the
seed. A seed transverse to the flow therefore never folds, and its count is
the flow parameter that carries the seed through $p$, which is single-valued
unless the flow's orbits close --- the rotational part --- in which case the
count returns to itself plus the period around the fixed point, and the
family winds. The one-parameter subgroups of the similarity group are
generated by a translation, a rotation about a point, a dilation about a
point, or a rotation and dilation about the same point, and that is the list.
Parallel lines, radial pencils, geometric rings and log spirals are the four
orbit families of a line through, or a circle about, the fixed point; the
Archimedean spiral and the wave are orbit families of other seeds; the
concentric families of a gauge are fronts.
\end{proof}

Fronts are where the fold lives. A front is a family of parallel curves of a
convex gauge, each member a dilate of the gauge walking and turning by a
fixed step, and its members touch one another where the front's normal speed
vanishes.

\begin{lemma}[Fold law]
\label{lem:foldlaw}
For a front whose member $n$ has support function $H_n$, gauge radius
$\rad_n$, and centre $c(n)$, the boundary-of-member map
$(u,n)\mapsto H_n(u)\,\hat u+H_n'(u)\,\hat u^{\perp}$ has Jacobian determinant
$-(H_n+H_n'')\,\partial_n H_n$ with $H_n+H_n''\ge0$ for a convex member. The
family folds exactly where the normal speed vanishes:
\[
  s\,H(w)\;-\;\rad_n\,\theta\,H'(w)\;+\;\langle c'(n),\hat u\rangle\;=\;0,
  \qquad w=u-n\theta .
\]
\end{lemma}

The proof is in the companion; it is the computation of the Jacobian of the
support-function parametrisation, and the factor $H+H''$ is the radius of
curvature of a convex member, which is nonnegative. What matters here is that
the discriminant of Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} is computed, and that its
zero set has closed-form corollaries.

\begin{corollary}[Rotation folds everything but circles]
\label{cor:rotation}
With $\delta=0$ and $\theta\ne0$ the family folds at gauge radius
$\rad^\star=s/(\theta L)$, $L=\sup_w|H'(w)/H(w)|$, and nowhere before. For the
regular $k$-gon $L=\tan(\pi/k)$ and the first crossings appear at the vertices,
at Euclidean radius $R^\star=s/(\theta\sin(\pi/k))$; for an ellipse with
semi-axes $a\ge b$, $L=(a^2-b^2)/2ab$. For the circle $L=0$. Onsets measured
by brute nesting tests are within \foldWorstOnsetPct\% of these radii.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[The Mach condition]
\label{cor:mach}
With $\theta=0$ the family folds if and only if $\rad(-\delta)>s$: the drift
constant $m=\rad(-\delta)$ is a Mach number. Subsonic families nest forever,
supersonic ones fold from their first member along straight wake lines, and the
marginal drift $m=s$ is the sonic boundary.
\end{corollary}

The Mach reading is not a metaphor. A front whose centre moves by $\delta$ per
member while its radius grows by $s$ is a source moving at speed
$\rad(-\delta)$ in the gauge's own metric through a medium in which its wave
spreads at speed $s$; the fold is the shock, the wake lines are its Mach
cone, and the onset index of a rotating walk, at which every rotating walk
eventually goes supersonic, is where the centre's speed
$\lVert\delta\rVert\sqrt{1+n^2\theta^2}$ crosses $s$.

Whether a fold can be inverted in closed form is a question of arithmetic,
and the answer draws the boundary of the tool's fast paths.

\begin{proposition}[The reach of closed forms]
\label{prop:reach}
With $\theta=0$ the residual $h_p(\nu)$ is convex in $\nu$ for every gauge, so
translation is one closed form per gauge. If $\theta=2\pi a/b$ in lowest terms
the family splits into $b$ translated subfamilies, each convex, so the inverse
is $b$ closed forms (exact against brute force at $\theta=4\pi/7$). That is the
only mechanism a formula has: for any $\theta\ne0$ the interpolated incidence
$\{(p,\nu):h_p(\nu)=0\}$ is not semialgebraic, since a semialgebraic incidence
would force $\nu\mapsto\nu\lVert\delta\rVert\cos(\nu\theta+\beta)$ to be
algebraic over $\R(\nu)$. Beyond it the count is multivalued, with branches
through a point growing linearly in radius (\foldBranchCounts).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Without rotation the residual $h_p(\nu)=\rad(p-\nu\delta)-\nu s-\varphi$ is a
convex gauge of an affine function of $\nu$ minus a linear one, hence convex,
and a convex function has one crossing per side, found by a solve on the
active facet. A rational twist $2\pi a/b$ makes the frame angle
$\nu\theta\bmod2\pi$ take $b$ values, constant on each residue class of $\nu$
modulo $b$, and on a class the family is a translated one. For an irrational
twist the set of frames is dense, and the centre's coordinate
$\nu\lVert\delta\rVert\cos(\nu\theta+\beta)$ is a transcendental function of
$\nu$; a semialgebraic incidence would express it algebraically by
eliminating $p$, which the definable-centre extraction of the companion's
appendix rules out. The branch count is the number of members within a
gauge's reach of a point, which grows with the radius because the fan of a
rotating front widens linearly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Defects]
\label{prop:defects}
A circle-valued field of integer amount $A$ composed into a layer's index
changes the monodromy of $\Phi$: each zero of winding number $q$ ends $Aq$
fringes, the signed count of fringe endings inside any loop is $A$ times the
enclosed charge, and the fringe law fails inside the core $r^\star=2As/\pi$
where $\het$ crosses $\tfrac14$. Measured on \defProbes\ probes with charges up
to \defChargeMax, exact; the core law within $\defCoreWorst$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Adding $A\,\vartheta/2\pi$ to a layer's index, with $\vartheta$ the polar
angle about a point, adds $A$ to the index's monodromy around that point and
nothing to it elsewhere, so the difference character $D=\idx_1-\idx_2$ against
an unmodulated twin gains a winding of $A$: its level sets, the fringes, must
end $A$ times inside any loop enclosing the point, and the count is additive
over enclosed points by the homomorphism of Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}.
Near the point the field's gradient is $A/2\pi r$ against a carrier gradient
of $1/s$, so the merit of the difference character is $\het=As/2\pi r$, which
crosses $\tfrac14$ at $r^\star=2As/\pi$; inside, the beat is no slower than
the carriers and the fringe law has nothing to describe.
\end{proof}

Defects are the winding rung made by hand, and they close the taxonomy from
above: a field can lift a family from the exact rung to the winding rung by a
quantized amount, and a non-integer amount tears the drawing along the
field's branch cut --- the tear is real, and it carries the fractional
winding --- but no field can fold a family, because folding is a property of
$\partial_\nu F$ and a field never touches it.
