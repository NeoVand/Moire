\section{Law I: selection, the arithmetic of the visible beat}
\label{sec:selection}

Every integer character is present in a drawing. The question is which one
the eye picks out, and the answer has two ingredients that the classical
theory keeps apart. One is geometry: how slowly the character's pullback
varies against the carriers that form it. The other is amplitude: how much ink
the character carries, which for a stroke profile decays with the harmonic.
The visible beat minimises their product.

\begin{definition}[Weighted merit]
\label{def:merit}
For a pair with index gradients $g_1,g_2$ and a character $(a,b)$ with $ab\ne0$,
\[
  \het_{a,b}\;=\;\frac{|a g_1+b g_2|}{\tfrac12\,|a g_1-b g_2|}\;\cdot\;|ab|,
\]
the heterodyne ratio of the beat against the carriers that form it, priced by
the harmonics that carry it. The visible character is the minimiser;
$\het<\tfrac14$ is the fringe regime.
\end{definition}

The heterodyne ratio is the classical criterion of the fringe regime, and it
is scale-free: a beat is visible when its period is long against the carriers
that form it, at any zoom. The weight is what makes the minimum well posed.
Without it every real pitch ratio has arbitrarily good rational
approximations, and the slowest character at any point is some $(a,b)$ of
enormous order carried by harmonics no stroke has; with it, comparable
slowness resolves toward the lower order, and the minimum over $\Z^2$ is
finite and stable.

\begin{proposition}[Selection is reduction]
\label{prop:selection}
The minimiser of $\het_{a,b}$ over $\Z^2\setminus\{ab=0\}$ lies in the window
$\{i\,s+j\,t : |i|\le3,\ j\in\{1,2\}\}$ of a Lagrange--Gauss reduced basis
$(s,t)$ of the gradient lattice $\{a g_1+b g_2\}$. For parallel carriers of
pitch ratio $x$ the record-setting characters are the continued-fraction
convergents of $x$ (\selRecordsMatch\ records over \selRatios\ ratios, all
convergents); on \selTrials\ random pairs an order cap $|a|,|b|\le2$ misses
\selCapMiss\ of the visible fringes a brute-force search over all orders
finds, and the window misses \selShipMiss.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The set $\{a g_1+b g_2\}$ is a lattice in $\R^2$, and Lagrange--Gauss reduction
finds its shortest vector in a handful of steps. The weighted minimiser need
not be a shortest vector --- a longer lattice vector can carry smaller integer
coordinates and pay a smaller weight --- but it lies within a bounded window
of the reduced basis, because outside the window either the length or the
weight exceeds the short vector's merit. In one dimension the lattice is
$\{a/s_1-b/s_2\}$, and a character that beats every lower-order one is a best
approximation of $s_1/s_2$ priced by $ab$ rather than by $b$ alone; the records
are the convergents, as measured.
\end{proof}

Selection is per pixel. On a pair whose local pitch ratio varies, the rational
stations become places: along the axis between two radial fans the ratio
sweeps the reals, each rational the weight admits owns a commensurate pocket,
and the fringe skeleton in Figure~\ref{fig:stations} is the convergent ladder
as geography. Badly approximable directions are deserts, and the golden ratio
is the worst approximable of all, which is why the printed carriers of this
paper sit at the golden slope: it is the angle at which a hairline family
beats least against the page's raster.

\subsection{Duty nulls}
\label{sec:dutynull}

Because the ink potential of an overlay is a product of one-coordinate
profiles, its coefficient at a character is a product of harmonics,
$\hat I(a,b)=\hat g_1(a)\,\hat g_2(b)$, and a station is carried by exactly the
harmonics its integers name. That makes a prediction the classical theory
does not: the visibility of a fringe system depends on the strokes' duty, and
vanishes at rational duties.

\begin{theorem}[Duty nulls]
\label{thm:dutynull}
The visible station of a $p{:}q$ pair (coarse\,:\,fine) is the character
$(q,-p)$, carried by the coarse family's $q$-th profile harmonic and the fine
family's $p$-th. A stroke of duty $d$ has vanishing $q$-th harmonic exactly when
$qd\in\Z$, so the station is extinguished at rational coarse duties and
nowhere else. Measured on the station over strips holding a whole number of
every period: the $2{:}1$ null at $d=\tfrac12$ is $\dutyNullDepthTwo\times$ below
its neighbours, the $3{:}1$ nulls at $\tfrac13$ and $\tfrac23$ are
$\dutyNullDepthThreeLow\times$ and $\dutyNullDepthThreeHigh\times$, and away from
the nulls the amplitudes track $|\sin(q\pi d)/q\pi|$ within \dutyNullLawPct\%,
the anti-alias ramp's softening.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For pitches $s_{\mathrm c}\approx(p/q)\,s_{\mathrm f}$ the slow character is
the one with $q/s_{\mathrm c}\approx p/s_{\mathrm f}$, that is $(q,-p)$, and by
the product structure its coefficient is
$\hat g_{\mathrm c}(q)\,\hat g_{\mathrm f}(-p)$. A centred pulse of duty $d$ has
$\hat g(n)=\sin(n\pi d)/n\pi$, which vanishes if and only if $nd\in\Z$.
Symmetric anti-alias ramps convolve the pulse with an even kernel, which
multiplies each harmonic by a real factor and moves no zero.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:dutynull} is the theorem measured. The corollary is fringe
spectroscopy in reverse: find the duty at which a station dies and the
stroke's duty has been measured to the precision of the null. It is also a
warning about what ``the fringe'' is. A first version of this experiment
projected onto $(1,-2)$ in the wrong order of the same pair --- a coefficient
with a period of five world units, real, product-of-harmonics like every
coefficient, and no fringe --- and measured a null of it; the slowness gate of
\S\ref{sec:observer}'s experiment now stands where that mistake was made.
Hard ink makes the null observer-proof and soft ink does not, which is
Corollary~\ref{cor:hardink}.

\subsection{The visibility spectrum of a real number}
\label{sec:visibility}

\begin{definition}[Visibility spectrum]
\label{def:visibility}
For $x>0$, the visibility spectrum is the staircase
$\nu_x(N)=\min\{\,|p-qx|\cdot pq:\ p,q\ge1,\ pq\le N\,\}$: best approximation
with denominators priced by the product $pq$ rather than by $q$. Its record
holders are the visible stations of a $1{:}x$ pair.
\end{definition}

The classical theory of best approximation prices a rational $p/q$ by its
denominator, and its constants --- Hurwitz's $1/\sqrt5$, the Lagrange
spectrum --- describe how well every real can be approximated at that price.
A stroke prices $p/q$ by $pq$, because both harmonics have to be paid for, and
the staircase of records under that price is what the eye sees as a pitch
ratio is tuned: a station appears where a record is set and holds until the
next. The records are still convergents (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}),
but the heights and the spacing of the staircase are not the classical
constants, and we know of no name for the object in the number-theoretic
literature. We state it as a definition and leave its arithmetic open.
