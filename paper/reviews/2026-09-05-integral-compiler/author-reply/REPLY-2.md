## 7. Done since the above: the analytic shift provider

`shiftAnalytic` in `paper/tools/exp/fjet.mjs` is now the compiler's default for a shift function that is a product of one sine or cosine per S axis. One axis: $Q(\theta;\ell) = \sum_j o_j\,c_{\ell-j}(\theta)$ with $c_n(\theta) = J_n(\theta)$ for a sine and $i^nJ_n(\theta)$ for a cosine, in the compiler's own $\theta$, as you specified. Two axes: $T_1(a)T_2(b) = \tfrac12[\epsilon_1U_1(a-b) + \epsilon_2U_2(a+b)]$, so the coefficient of $e^{i(na+mb)}$ is $u_{1,p}(\epsilon_1\theta/2)\,u_{2,q}(\epsilon_2\theta/2)$ with $p = (n-m)/2$, $q = (n+m)/2$, products of two Bessel functions at half the argument, all four sine and cosine combinations. The other closures $O$ (the shading relu) are sampled once on a 64-point grid per axis and transformed; their spectrum, truncated at $10^{-5}$ of its largest term, convolves the Bessel coefficients. $Q'$ and $Q''$ come from adjacent orders; the Bessel functions from Miller's backward recurrence in double precision; the sideband window is $\lceil\Theta\rceil + 12 + 3\Theta^{1/3}$ instead of a fixed 16, and the mixed-frequency enumeration is the existing one.

Gate `fjet-bessel.mjs`, against the tables: $1.5\times10^{-8}$ on $Q, Q', Q''$ (Float32 storage) for one and two axes, sines and cosines, with and without a smooth $O$, where the 64-point table is sound; $5\times10^{-9}$ against a 1024-point table at $\theta = 40$ and $64$, where the 64-point one aliases as you measured.

On the benchmark, at the published amplitude and at twice it: on every ripple pixel the provider reproduces the table's value to five decimals at the same cost, keeps sidebands to order 18 to 21 where the table's window stopped at 16, and at a Bessel argument of 33 agrees with the table to $10^{-5}$, the table's aliasing there being $2\times10^{-6}$ by your measurement; so the sweep's amplitude range is safe on either route, and beyond it only on this one. On the bumps the shading's spectrum has 125 terms on two axes and the per-request convolution loses to the table's fixed price: 96 to 179 thousand coefficient requests a pixel over 4 to 8 thousand distinct $\theta$, $1.4$ to $2.4$ s against $0.4$ to $0.9$ s. Truncating that spectrum harder is harmless ($10^{-4}$ relative changes a pixel by $3\times10^{-8}$) but not enough, so two-axis shifts with more than 64 spectrum terms stay on the tables, whose torus grid now grows with the argument (64 up to $\theta\approx34$, then 128), which removes the aliasing on that route too. Both routes are exact for the model where they run; the table interpolates in $\theta$ at the $10^{-6}$ level.

Two things this settles for the demo. The one-axis warp (ripples) has closed-form per-pixel coefficients and needs no table at all on the GPU. The two-axis warp (bumps) is bounded not by the coefficients but by the request count: a hundred thousand coefficient evaluations a pixel is not a real-time path whatever produces them, and the GPU form must bound the retained recipes first; $O$'s spectrum does not depend on the view, so it is a per-shader constant table, and the per-recipe work is a short Bessel product sum. That bound on the recipe set is the next thing I need to understand, and your float32 Bessel evaluation is what it would run on.
