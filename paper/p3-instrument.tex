\section{The instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}

Every result in this paper was found in a drawing tool before it was
proved, and measured in the tool after. \Moire\ is a web application that
draws superpositions of families the way this paper describes them: each
layer is a count $\cnt_i$ and a stroke profile, evaluated at every pixel
--- by a formula for the exact and winding rungs, by a certified search for
the fold rung --- and the picture on the torus is looked up, never
rasterised in between, so a superposition has no resolution of its own and
can be zoomed without inventing or destroying a beat. Fourteen families
ship: lines, rings, rays, polygons, spirals of two kinds, waves, parabolas,
hyperbolas, three lattices and tilings, and the walking families, with a
field language for adding any function of position to a count.

Three of its views are this paper's theorems running. The \emph{pooled}
view is Theorem~\ref{thm:sweep}: the slide average, integrated in closed
form --- every swept profile is piecewise cubic with corners known from the
count, so the average is exact at every zoom, with no sampling and no
noise, and it runs at the frame rate. The \emph{ratio} view is
Definition~\ref{def:merit}: the priced merit at every pixel, reduced per
pixel, drawn as a map that is dark where a beat must form. A
\emph{square-law} toggle is Theorem~\ref{thm:universality}: the drawing
squared before it is averaged, so that Corollary~\ref{cor:soft} can be
watched as a null reopening when the zoom widens the strokes' ramps. Every
view is a measurement with a gate: the tool's renders are checked against
golden images through the real graphics device on every change, its solver
is checked against a brute-force twin, and every number in this paper is
written into the text by a script that fails if the experiment does. The
tool, its source, and every script named in this paper are public
\todo{URL}; the systems paper describes the engineering.
