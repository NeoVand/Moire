\subsection{The fringe law}
\label{app:fringe}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:fringe}. Recall the setting. Layer $i$ has index field
$\idx_i$, and its ink at a point $q$ comes from thresholding the Euclidean distance
to the nearest member,
\begin{equation}
  d_i(q)=\frac{\mathrm{pd}\big(\idx_i(q),1\big)}{\lVert\nabla\idx_i(q)\rVert},
  \label{eq:appdist}
\end{equation}
against the half-width $h_i$ with an antialiasing band $a$, giving
$\alpha_i(q)=1-\mathrm{smoothstep}(h_i-a,\,h_i+a,\,d_i(q))$. Here
$\mathrm{pd}(x,1)=\min_{n\in\Z}\lvert x-n\rvert$ is the distance to the nearest
integer. The two layers share a colour and composite as a union,
$\alpha=\alpha_1+\alpha_2-\alpha_1\alpha_2$.

\paragraph{Step 1: move the threshold into index units.}
Substituting \eqref{eq:appdist}, the condition $d_i\le h_i$ is
$\mathrm{pd}(\idx_i,1)\le h_i\lVert\nabla\idx_i\rVert$. Writing
$w_i=h_i\lVert\nabla\idx_i\rVert$ and $b_i=a\lVert\nabla\idx_i\rVert$ for the
half-width and band \emph{measured in index units}, and
\begin{equation}
  A_i(v)=1-\mathrm{smoothstep}\big(w_i-b_i,\;w_i+b_i,\;\mathrm{pd}(v,1)\big),
  \label{eq:appprofile}
\end{equation}
we have exactly $\alpha_i(q)=A_i(\idx_i(q))$. This is the only place the eikonal
factor of \S\ref{sec:eikonal} is used, and it is used twice: once to make the
threshold comparable to a length, and once to make $w_i$ and $b_i$ constants rather
than functions of $q$. The hypothesis that $\lVert\nabla\idx_i\rVert$ be
sensibly constant on $S$ is therefore not cosmetic, and the verification in
\S\ref{sec:fringe} excludes samples where it varies by more than $15\%$ across a
period for exactly this reason.

\paragraph{Step 2: reparameterise the segment by index.}
Let $g=\tfrac12(\nabla\idx_1+\nabla\idx_2)$, let $u=g/\lVert g\rVert$, let
$T=1/\lVert g\rVert$, and parameterise $S$ by arclength $t\in[-T/2,T/2]$, so
$q(t)=p+tu$. Because both families are local foliations on $S$ with gradients close
to $g$, the map $t\mapsto v=\idx_1(q(t))$ is a diffeomorphism onto an interval of
length
\begin{equation}
  \lVert\nabla\idx_1\rVert\,T\;=\;\frac{\lVert\nabla\idx_1\rVert}{\lVert g\rVert}
  \;=\;1+O(\het),
\end{equation}
since $\nabla\idx_1=g+\tfrac12\nabla D$ and $\het=\lVert\nabla D\rVert/\lVert
g\rVert$. Up to the same order the parameterisation is affine. So averaging over $S$
in $t$ is, to first order in $\het$, averaging over one unit period in $v$; and
because both $A_1$ and $A_2$ are $1$-periodic, the choice of representative period
does not matter.

\paragraph{Step 3: the constant case.}
By definition $\idx_2=\idx_1-D$, so in the new variable the second layer's
argument is $v-D$. If $D$ is constant on $S$, equal to $D(p)$, then
\begin{align}
  \bar\alpha(p)
  &=\int_0^1\Big[A_1(v)+A_2\big(v-D(p)\big)-A_1(v)A_2\big(v-D(p)\big)\Big]\mathrm{d}v
  \notag\\
  &=\Phi\big(D(p)\big),
\end{align}
which is \eqref{eq:phi}. Every dependence on $p$ has been absorbed into the single
number $D(p)$: the individual index fields have disappeared, and with them any
notion of periodicity, orientation, or curvature of either family. This is the
content of the theorem.

\paragraph{Step 4: the hard-edged limit.}
Let $b_i\to0$, so $A_i$ becomes the indicator of
$\{\mathrm{pd}(v,1)\le w_i\}$, an interval of length $2w_i$ per period. Then
$\int_0^1 A_i=2w_i$, and $\int_0^1A_1(v)A_2(v-g)\,\mathrm{d}v$ is the overlap of
two intervals of half-widths $w_1$ and $w_2$ whose centres are $\mathrm{pd}(g,1)$
apart, which is $\max(0,\,w_1+w_2-\mathrm{pd}(g,1))$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(g)=2w_1+2w_2-\max\!\big(0,\;w_1+w_2-\mathrm{pd}(g,1)\big),
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:tent}. It is piecewise linear in $\mathrm{pd}(g,1)$ with slope
$1$ while the strokes overlap and slope $0$ after they separate, so it is a tent
with a plateau, minimised at $g\in\Z$ at the value $2\max(w_1,w_2)$ and maximised
at $2w_1+2w_2$ wherever $\mathrm{pd}(g,1)\ge w_1+w_2$. Note that the plateau is
reached strictly inside the period whenever $w_1+w_2<\tfrac12$, which is the
regime the tool operates in and the reason thickening strokes eventually stops
changing the fringe.

\paragraph{Step 5: the affine case, and why the error is first order.}
Suppose now that $D$ is affine on $S$ rather than constant. In the index variable
write $D=D(p)+\kappa\,(v-v_p)$, where $\kappa$ is the change in $D$ per unit index,
so that $\lvert\kappa\rvert=\het+O(\het^2)$ by the definition of $\het$. Put
$F(v,\gamma)=A_1(v)+A_2(v-\gamma)-A_1(v)A_2(v-\gamma)$, so that
$\Phi(\gamma)=\int_0^1F(v,\gamma)\,\mathrm{d}v$. Averaging over the period and
expanding in $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
  \bar\alpha(p)=\Phi\big(D(p)\big)
  +\kappa\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\!\! (v-v_p)\,\partial_\gamma F\big(v,D(p)\big)\,\mathrm{d}v
  +O(\kappa^2).
  \label{eq:appfirst}
\end{equation}
The integral is bounded: $\partial_\gamma F(v,\gamma)=A_2'(v-\gamma)\,(A_1(v)-1)$,
and $\lvert A_1-1\rvert\le1$ while $\int_0^1\lvert A_2'\rvert=2$ for the
single-bump profile \eqref{eq:appprofile}, so the correction is at most
$\lvert\kappa\rvert/2$ in absolute value. Hence
$\bar\alpha(p)=\Phi(D(p))+O(\het)$, as claimed.

We stress that the first-order term does not vanish identically. It is odd in
$(v-v_p)$ against a factor that is not even, so there is no symmetry argument that
kills it, and one should expect a genuine $O(\het)$ bias --- a slight asymmetry of
the fringe profile, leaning in the direction the carrier is changing. This is
visible in the data: the measured light fringes of Table~\ref{tab:fringe} sit at
mean index gap $\fringeLight$ from an integer rather than at $0$. The bound above
says that residual should be a fraction of $\het$, and at the regime boundary
$\het=1/4$ it is.
\qed

\subsection{Remaining proofs}

constant in \S\ref{sec:drift}, the two-sided envelope as
Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}, the sufficient interval as Theorem~\ref{thm:window},
the Lipschitz skip bound as Lemma~\ref{lem:lipschitz}, and the closed form for
translated polygons as Proposition~\ref{prop:closed}. Each proof is short enough
that separating it from its statement would cost the reader more than it saves.
