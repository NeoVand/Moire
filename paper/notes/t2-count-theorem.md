# The T2 count theorem: three branches, certified cutoffs, material work kept separate

Consolidated from the bridge exchange of 2026-09-06 (#109 to #143) and section 12 of beyond-count-maps.md, for the collaborator's audit. Attribution is by message: the density lemma and the retention-level repair are theirs (#98, #118), the counts and the crossover are mine (#115), the needle lower bound is theirs (#113), the mixed conditioning identities are mine (#134), the lattice-line upper bound, the accounting, the oriented-edge evaluation and the conditional cutoff are theirs (#139, #140, #143), the wrapped edge count is mine (below). Theory only; the arithmetic model is exact reals with Gaussian CDF, density and complex error function as primitives, and precision is a separate obligation.

## Setting

A bounded material g on T^2, 0 <= g <= 1, with Fourier coefficients g_hat(n). A pixel whose pushforward onto the torus is the wrapped Gaussian with mean theta_0 and covariance Q, symmetric positive definite with eigenvalues lambda_1 <= lambda_2, s = sqrt(det Q), condition number kappa = lambda_2 / lambda_1. A tolerance eps, R = sqrt(2 ln(1 / eps)) so that the footprint's mass outside u^T Q^-1 u <= R^2 is at most eps. The claim is about the number of terms each branch evaluates and the certificate each carries; the work per term is the node's, stated separately.

## Branch S, spectral

Retain E_c = {n in Z^2 : n^T Q n <= c} and evaluate sum over E_c of g_hat(n) exp(i n . theta_0 - n^T Q n / 2). The error is at most (1 / 2) sqrt(sum over n outside E_c of exp(-n^T Q n)) for any measurable 0 <= g <= 1 (the density lemma: the residual has zero mass, then Cauchy-Schwarz and Parseval on the wrapped Gaussian density; #98). The count is at most pi c / s + sqrt(2) pi sqrt(c / lambda_1) + pi / 2 (unit squares around lattice points inside the ellipse's 1 / sqrt(2) neighbourhood, Steiner, the perimeter of the ellipse at most that of its circumscribed disc). The retention level must not be set from the determinant (a needle has a theta sum of order its length at determinant one); with 0 < eta < 1 and M = R / (2 eta) + (pi / (2 sqrt eta)) sqrt(R) kappa^{1/4} + pi / 2, the level c = max(1, [2 ln(1 / (2 eps)) + ln M] / (1 - eta)) makes the squared tail at most 4 eps^2 on the branch where it is used, by a layer-cake bound of the theta sum from the count itself (#118). Material work: one coefficient per term, a table lookup with its preprocessing and memory stated, or O(E) per coefficient from the edge formula of a polygon with E edges per period (#140).

## Branch C, spatial

Sum the exact integral of g against the Gaussian over every cell of 2 pi Z^2 that meets the footprint ellipse u^T Q^-1 u <= R^2. The error is at most eps from the footprint's tail. The count is at most R^2 s / (4 pi) + sqrt(2) R sqrt(lambda_2) + 2 pi (each such cell lies in the ellipse's 2 pi sqrt(2) neighbourhood, Steiner). Material work: the node's cell integral, closed form for polygons and conics, the enclosure for thresholds of smooth fields, a filtered lookup for textures.

## The two-sided bound and its lower bound

Choose S when s >= s_0 = 2 pi sqrt(c) / R and C otherwise. Then min(count_S, count_C) <= R sqrt(c) / 2 + 2 sqrt(pi R sqrt(c)) kappa^{1/4} + 2 pi for every Q, with c = c(eps, kappa) independent of the overall scale of Q (#115, #118). The kappa^{1/4} is not an artefact: for Q = diag(L^2, L^-2) the spectral ellipse holds (0, k) for |k| <= L sqrt(c) and the footprint holds the cell centres (2 pi j, 0) for |j| <= R L / (2 pi), so both counts are at least linear in L = kappa^{1/4} (#113). At eps = 1e-3 the leading term is about seven and the whole bound about 27 for an isotropic footprint, about 150 at aspect ratio 100.

## Branch M, mixed

Take A = [a_1 a_2] in GL(2, Z) with a_2 primitive at angle eps_a from the long axis e_2 and a_1 a Bezout complement (a torus automorphism; an eigenrotation is not one). In phi = A^-1 theta the footprint has Var(phi_1) = a_2^perp^T Q a_2^perp = q^2 s D across the vector and Var(phi_2 | phi_1) = 1 / (a_2^T Q^-1 a_2) = s / (q^2 D) along it, with q = |a_2| and D = cos^2(eps_a) / sqrt(kappa) + sqrt(kappa) sin^2(eps_a) (#134, checked in #139). Expand g in characters of phi_2 only and condition on phi_1: the pixel value is sum over n_2 of the integral over phi_1 of g_{n_2}(phi_1) exp(i n_2 mu(phi_1)) exp(-n_2^2 Var(phi_2 | phi_1) / 2) p(phi_1) dphi_1, mu affine and p Gaussian of variance Var(phi_1).

Cutoff and certificate: drop |n_2| > N. For a bounded material the change of the conditional mean at any phi_1 is at most (1 / 2) sqrt(sum over |n| > N of exp(-Var(phi_2 | phi_1) n^2)), uniformly in phi_1 and in the conditional mean (the density lemma in one dimension, #143); the transverse truncation to the reached cells adds the Gaussian tail. At balanced scale (s bounded above and below) with a bounded-denominator Dirichlet vector a_2 of length O(kappa^{1/4}), Var(phi_2 | phi_1) has a positive lower bound and N is bounded for fixed error.

Counts: cells across R sqrt(Var(phi_1)) / pi + 1; characters along 2 N + 1; their product about (R sqrt(c) / pi) q^2 D up to the +1 terms, a constant at q = kappa^{1/4} and eps_a = 1 / q^2.

Material work for a polygon picture: the line phi_1 = const in the torus is theta = phi_1 a_1 + phi_2 a_2, closed and winding. Map the oriented polygon boundary into phi coordinates and cut it at the seams phi_1, phi_2 in 2 pi Z: an edge of theta-length ell maps to a segment with |Delta phi_1| = |a_2^perp . Delta theta| <= q ell and |Delta phi_2| = |a_1^perp . Delta theta| <= |a_1| ell <= q ell for a reduced a_1, so it is cut into at most 1 + q ell / pi pieces, and a picture with E edges of length O(2 pi) per period has O(q E) wrapped edge pieces per fundamental domain. For each retained n_2 and each wrapped edge piece, the crossing of the line with the piece has phi_2 affine in phi_1 over the piece's transverse span, so the piece's contribution to the partial coefficient is (sign) exp(-i n_2 phi_2(phi_1)) / (-2 pi i n_2), the exponential of an affine function, or the affine function itself for n_2 = 0; multiplied by the conditional phase and weight and integrated against p over the span it is one complex Gaussian interval primitive (a difference of complex error functions), or a CDF and density difference for n_2 = 0 (#143). This integrates oriented edges directly and never expands the intervals' sums of active edges, which can be quadratic. So the work per retained mode and reached transverse period is O(q E) primitives, and the branch's total is O((R sqrt(Var(phi_1)) / pi + 1)(2 N + 1) q E).

## The three-way minimum

All three branches are needed: Q = diag(L^-4, L^-2), kappa = L^2, has Theta(L^3) spectral modes and Theta(L) characters even on the aligned mixed branch, while the spatial branch reaches O(1) cells (#139). At balanced scale the collaborator's lattice-line bound (#139) gives the spectral count a real upper bound of O(1 + kappa^{1/4} / q) for the same Dirichlet a_2: the populated lines a_2 . n = j number at most 2 floor(sqrt(c a_2^T Q^-1 a_2)) + 1 = O(1) since a_2^T Q^-1 a_2 = O(1) / s^2, and each holds O(1 + kappa^{1/4} / q) points by the lattice spacing q along the line against the ellipse chord. With the mixed material work O(q E), the smaller of S and M is O(1 + kappa^{1/8} sqrt(E)) in terms plus lookups when the coefficients come from a table at unit cost, and O(E (1 + kappa^{1/8})) when each is built at O(E) from the edge formula (#140). For a footprint aligned with a lattice direction (q small, eps_a = 0), which is an axis-aligned tiling at its horizon and the regime where the kernels paid for edge pairs, the mixed branch costs O(E) against the two-sided Theta(kappa^{1/4}).
