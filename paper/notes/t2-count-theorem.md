# The T2 count theorem: three branches, certified cutoffs, material work kept separate

Consolidated from the bridge exchange of 2026-09-06 (#109 to #143) and section 12 of beyond-count-maps.md, for the collaborator's audit. Attribution is by message: the density lemma and the retention-level repair are theirs (#98, #118), the counts and the crossover are mine (#115), the needle lower bound is theirs (#113), the mixed conditioning identities are mine (#134), the lattice-line upper bound, the accounting, the oriented-edge evaluation and the conditional cutoff are theirs (#139, #140, #143), the wrapped edge count is mine (below). Theory only; the arithmetic model is exact reals with Gaussian CDF, density and complex error function as primitives, and precision is a separate obligation.

## Setting

A bounded material g on T^2, 0 <= g <= 1, with Fourier coefficients g_hat(n). A pixel whose pushforward onto the torus is the wrapped Gaussian with mean theta_0 and covariance Q, symmetric positive definite with eigenvalues lambda_1 <= lambda_2, s = sqrt(det Q), condition number kappa = lambda_2 / lambda_1. A tolerance eps, R = sqrt(2 ln(1 / eps)) so that the footprint's mass outside u^T Q^-1 u <= R^2 is at most eps. The claim is about the number of terms each branch evaluates and the certificate each carries; the work per term is the node's, stated separately.

## Branch S, spectral

Retain E_c = {n in Z^2 : n^T Q n <= c} and evaluate sum over E_c of g_hat(n) exp(i n . theta_0 - n^T Q n / 2). The error is at most (1 / 2) sqrt(sum over n outside E_c of exp(-n^T Q n)) for any measurable 0 <= g <= 1 (the density lemma: the residual has zero mass, then Cauchy-Schwarz and Parseval on the wrapped Gaussian density; #98). The count is at most pi c / s + sqrt(2) pi sqrt(c / lambda_1) + pi / 2 (unit squares around lattice points inside the ellipse's 1 / sqrt(2) neighbourhood, Steiner, the perimeter of the ellipse at most that of its circumscribed disc). The retention level must not be set from the determinant (a needle has a theta sum of order its length at determinant one); with 0 < eta < 1 and M = R / (2 eta) + (pi / (2 sqrt eta)) sqrt(R) kappa^{1/4} + pi / 2, the level c = max(1, [2 ln(1 / (2 eps)) + ln M] / (1 - eta)) makes the squared tail at most 4 eps^2 on the branch where it is used, by a layer-cake bound of the theta sum from the count itself (#118). Material work: one coefficient per term, a table lookup with its preprocessing and memory stated, or O(E) per coefficient from the edge formula of a polygon with E edges per period (#140).

## Branch C, spatial

Sum the exact integral of g against the Gaussian over every cell of 2 pi Z^2 that meets the footprint ellipse u^T Q^-1 u <= R^2. The error is at most eps from the footprint's tail. The count is at most R^2 s / (4 pi) + sqrt(2) R sqrt(lambda_2) + 2 pi (each such cell lies in the ellipse's 2 pi sqrt(2) neighbourhood, Steiner). Material work: the branch is conditional on a supplied certified cell integrator, whose primitive and cost enter the material work term (#148). For polygons the primitive is the bivariate normal over a wedge (Owen's T or an equivalent, one per edge pair), which is not among the one-dimensional primitives of the other branches; for conics a one-dimensional quadrature of the error function along the boundary with its own stated error; for thresholds of smooth fields the enclosure with its contract; for textures a filtered lookup with its error. The affine-edge formulas of the mixed branch do not transfer to an unrestricted two-dimensional Gaussian cell integral.

## The two-sided bound and its lower bound

Choose S when s >= s_0 = 2 pi sqrt(c) / R and C otherwise. Then min(count_S, count_C) <= R sqrt(c) / 2 + 2 sqrt(pi R sqrt(c)) kappa^{1/4} + 2 pi for every Q, with c = c(eps, kappa) independent of the overall scale of Q (#115, #118). The kappa^{1/4} is not an artefact: for Q = diag(L^2, L^-2) the spectral ellipse holds (0, k) for |k| <= L sqrt(c) and the footprint holds the cell centres (2 pi j, 0) for |j| <= R L / (2 pi), so both counts are at least linear in L = kappa^{1/4} (#113). At eps = 1e-3 with eta = 0.1 and the branch's own certificate carrying the whole eps (the density tail on S, the footprint tail on C, evaluation error reserved separately), the repaired level is c = 17.6 for an isotropic footprint and 19.1 at aspect ratio 100, the leading term is about eight, and the whole bound about 28 and about 160 respectively.

## Branch M, mixed

Take A = [a_1 a_2] in GL(2, Z) with a_2 primitive at angle eps_a from the long axis e_2 and a_1 a Bezout complement (a torus automorphism; an eigenrotation is not one). In phi = A^-1 theta the footprint has Var(phi_1) = a_2^perp^T Q a_2^perp = q^2 s D across the vector and Var(phi_2 | phi_1) = 1 / (a_2^T Q^-1 a_2) = s / (q^2 D) along it, with q = |a_2| and D = cos^2(eps_a) / sqrt(kappa) + sqrt(kappa) sin^2(eps_a) (#134, checked in #139). Expand g in characters of phi_2 only and condition on phi_1: the pixel value is sum over n_2 of the integral over phi_1 of g_{n_2}(phi_1) exp(i n_2 mu(phi_1)) exp(-n_2^2 Var(phi_2 | phi_1) / 2) p(phi_1) dphi_1, mu affine and p Gaussian of variance Var(phi_1).

Cutoff and certificate: drop |n_2| > N. Let H(phi_1) in [0, 1] be the true conditional mean and H_N its truncation, with |H - H_N| <= delta_N = (1 / 2) sqrt(sum over |n| > N of exp(-Var(phi_2 | phi_1) n^2)) uniformly in phi_1 and in the conditional mean (the density lemma in one dimension, #143). On the retained transverse cells U, |E H - int_U H_N p| <= delta_N P(U) + P(U^c) <= delta_N + tau: the transverse tail tau is charged on the bounded H, never on the signed H_N, and delta_N, tau and the evaluation error of the primitives are allocated to sum to eps (#148). At balanced scale (s bounded above and below) with a bounded-denominator Dirichlet vector a_2 of length O(kappa^{1/4}), Var(phi_2 | phi_1) has a positive lower bound and N is bounded for fixed error.

Counts: cells across ceil(R sqrt(Var(phi_1)) / pi) + 1 (the unrounded expression plus one can undercount); characters along 2 N + 1 with N about sqrt(c / Var(phi_2 | phi_1)); their leading product 2 R sqrt(c) q^2 D / pi, a constant at q = kappa^{1/4} and eps_a = 1 / q^2, the +1 terms carried separately (#149).

Material work for a polygon picture: the line phi_1 = const in the torus is theta = phi_1 a_1 + phi_2 a_2, closed and winding. Map the oriented polygon boundary into phi coordinates and cut it at the seams phi_1, phi_2 in 2 pi Z: an edge of theta-length ell maps to a segment with |Delta phi_1| = |a_2^perp . Delta theta| <= q ell and |Delta phi_2| = |a_1^perp . Delta theta| <= |a_1| ell <= q ell for a reduced a_1, so it is cut into at most 1 + ceil(|Delta phi_1| / 2 pi) + ceil(|Delta phi_2| / 2 pi) <= 3 + q ell / pi pieces (a tiny segment can still cross a seam, so the integer bound carries the ceilings), and a picture with E edges of length O(2 pi) per period has O(q E) wrapped edge pieces per fundamental domain. Here E counts an already resolved oriented boundary whose jump is constant along each edge, with intersections and compositing already charged; raw polygon input edges are not in general that representation (#149). Sweeping phi_2 from 0 to 2 pi along the line, g(phi_1, phi_2) = g(phi_1, 0) + sum over the crossings y_j(phi_1) < phi_2 of the jumps Delta g_j (plus one entering, minus one leaving), and periodicity makes the jumps sum to zero. Integrating gives the partial coefficients: for n_2 != 0, g_{n_2}(phi_1) = (1 / 2 pi) sum_j Delta g_j exp(-i n_2 y_j) / (i n_2), a sum over crossings only, the boundary term cancelling by periodicity; for n_2 = 0, g_0(phi_1) = g(phi_1, 0) - (1 / 2 pi) sum_j y_j Delta g_j, which needs the seam trace g(phi_1, 0) as well as the crossings, since a strip constant along the line, or the full material, has no crossings and a nonzero zero mode (the collaborator's #148; the first version of this note omitted the seam term). The seam phi_2 = 0 is itself a line in the torus, of winding |a_1| <= q, so the trace is piecewise constant in phi_1 with O(q E) breakpoints and a base fill bit for regions the boundary never enters. Each crossing y_j is affine in phi_1 over its edge piece's transverse span, so its contribution is the exponential of an affine function for n_2 != 0 and an affine function for n_2 = 0; multiplied by the conditional phase and weight and integrated against p over the span it is one complex Gaussian interval primitive (a difference of complex error functions), or a CDF and density difference for n_2 = 0, and the seam trace integrates by CDF differences over its constant pieces (#143, #148). This integrates oriented edges directly and never expands the intervals' sums of active edges, which can be quadratic. So the work per retained mode and reached transverse period is O(q E + 1) primitives, and the branch's total is O((R sqrt(Var(phi_1)) / pi + 1)(2 N + 1)(q E + 1)).

## The three-way minimum

All three branches are needed: Q = diag(L^-4, L^-2), kappa = L^2, has Theta(L^3) spectral modes and Theta(L) characters even on the aligned mixed branch, while the spatial branch reaches O(1) cells (#139). At balanced scale the collaborator's lattice-line bound (#139) gives the spectral count a real upper bound of O(1 + kappa^{1/4} / q) for the same Dirichlet a_2: the populated lines a_2 . n = j number at most 2 floor(sqrt(c a_2^T Q^-1 a_2)) + 1 = O(1) since a_2^T Q^-1 a_2 = O(1) / s^2, and each holds O(1 + kappa^{1/4} / q) points by the lattice spacing q along the line against the ellipse chord. The certified level c(eps, kappa) grows like log(2 + kappa) at fixed eps, so the spectral count on the lattice-line bound is O(c (1 + kappa^{1/4} / q)) while the mixed branch has bounded modes and periods from its conditional certificate and work O(q E + 1); the smaller of S and M is therefore O(c + sqrt(c kappa^{1/4} E)) in terms plus lookups when the coefficients come from a table at unit cost, and O(E (c + sqrt(c kappa^{1/4}))) when each is built at O(E) from the edge formula: a kappa^{1/8} exponent carrying a sqrt(log kappa) factor at fixed error, kept rather than hidden under a fixed cutoff (#140, #149). For a footprint aligned with a lattice direction (q small, eps_a = 0), which is an axis-aligned tiling at its horizon and the regime where the kernels paid for edge pairs, the mixed branch costs O(E) against the two-sided Theta(kappa^{1/4}).
