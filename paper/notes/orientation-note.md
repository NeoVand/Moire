# The orientation note: the pair tail of polygonal materials by edge orientation

2026-09-06. A theorem draft for the collaborator's audit (bridge #349 to #355). It refines the source tails of the two-factor composition certificate (composition-certificate.md, the collaborator's GAUSSIAN-LATTICE-PAIR.md) for materials that are piecewise constant on polygonal partitions: the pair mass dropped when the sources are cut at a harmonic radius M is bounded edge pair by edge pair, uniformly in orientation at the rate 1 / M, and for edge pairs with separated normals at the rate log M / M^2. Everything is in radian frequencies with the symbols of the pair note. Certified inequalities are marked as such; one sharper form is an asymptotic and is kept apart. Nothing here is a runtime cost.

## 1. Setting

A material A is a function on the plane with values in [0, 1], periodic on a lattice Lambda_A with cell area D_A, piecewise constant on a polygonal partition of the cell: finitely many edges e, each of length l_e, unit tangent t_e, unit normal n_e, and jump |jump_e| in [0, 1], the difference of the values on its two sides. Its Fourier series uses radian frequencies on the frequency lattice Lambda_A^*, whose cell area is D = (2 pi)^2 / D_A:

    A(x) = sum_{m in Lambda_A^*} a_m e^{i m . x},   a_m = (1 / D_A) int_cell A(x) e^{-i m . x} dx.

The covering radius rho of Lambda_A^* is the largest distance from a point of a fundamental cell to its centre (for a rectangular cell, half its diagonal). A second material B has the same data with the subscript B, edges f, frequency lattice Lambda_B^* with cell area D' = (2 pi)^2 / D_B and covering radius rho'.

The pixel keeps the beats m + n inside an output set E, and the pair note's neighbour bound is the count L_B = max over m of #{n in Lambda_B^* : m + n in E}; here N_E denotes that count, and r_E denotes the reach of E, so that every k in E has |k| <= r_E (for the ellipse k^T Sigma k <= R_out^2, r_E = R_out / sqrt(lambda_min(Sigma))). The one-sided dropped set at radius M is

    P_A(M) = {(m, n) : m in Lambda_A^*, |m| > M, n in Lambda_B^*, m + n in E},

and the quantity bounded is its mass sum_{P_A(M)} |a_m| |b_n|, which dominates the change of the filtered product from dropping those pairs (each pair lands at one beat and the damping is at most one). The mirror set P_B(M) with |n| > M is bounded by the same theorems with the roles exchanged. Pairs whose beat lies outside E are the pair note's output-tail allowance and are not counted here.

## 2. Lemma 1, the per-edge bound

For m in Lambda_A^*, m != 0,

    |a_m| <= sum_e |jump_e| g_e(m . t_e) / (D_A |m|),   g_e(s) = min(l_e, 2 / |s|).

Proof. Write A = sum_c v_c 1_{P_c} over the cells of the partition. For one cell, the divergence theorem with the field e^{-i m . x} m / (-i |m|^2), whose divergence is e^{-i m . x}, gives int_{P_c} e^{-i m . x} dx = (1 / (-i |m|^2)) sum_{edges of P_c} (m . n_out) I_e(m), I_e(m) = int_e e^{-i m . x} ds. Summing over cells, each edge appears once from each side with opposite outward normals, so D_A a_m = (1 / (-i |m|^2)) sum_e (v_right - v_left)(m . n_e) I_e(m) with n_e pointing from the right cell into the left one (the left cell's outward normal on e is -n_e and the right cell's is n_e); the sign matters for a coefficient oracle and not for the envelopes below [sign corrected after the collaborator's audit, #365]. Along the edge, m . x = m . p + s (m . t_e), so |I_e(m)| = |int_0^{l_e} e^{-i s (m . t_e)} ds| <= min(l_e, 2 / |m . t_e|). With |m . n_e| <= |m| the claim follows. Certified.

The bound is a star: inside the strip |m . t_e| <= 2 / l_e the e-term is |jump_e| l_e / (D_A |m|), and outside it |jump_e| 2 / (D_A |m| |m . t_e|), the inverse square with an angular factor. This is a quantitative finite-edge envelope and nothing more: the edge Fourier formula and its angular decay are Lemma 2.2 of Brandolini, Colzani and Travaglini (1997), the organization of interactions by spectral orientation is in Durand and others (2005), and a polygon's wavefront set contains the corner covectors as well as the edge conormals, so the result below is not a product criterion on wavefront sets but an envelope on a lattice, with constants.

## 3. Lemma 2, the counting lemma (the collaborator's, #353)

For a lattice Lambda with cell area D and covering radius rho, a unit vector t, and R, u > 0,

    #{m in Lambda : |m| <= 2R, |m . t| <= u} <= 4 (2R + rho)(u + rho) / D,   #{m in Lambda : |m| <= 2R} <= pi (2R + rho)^2 / D.

Proof. The translated fundamental cells centred at the counted points are disjoint, each of area D, and each lies inside the rectangle |x . t| <= u + rho, |x . n| <= 2R + rho (respectively inside the disc of radius 2R + rho), whose area bounds their number. Certified; no comparison of a point value with a cell supremum is used.

## 4. Lemma 3, the layer-cake sums on a dyadic annulus

For the annulus R < |m| <= 2R of Lambda_A^*, a unit vector t, a length l > 0 and a shift c >= 0, let f_{l,c}(s) = min(l, 2 / (|s| - c)_+) (the l branch when the denominator vanishes), and write A_R = 4 (2R + rho) / D and Psi(x) = x for x <= 1, Psi(x) = 1 + log x for x > 1. Then

    sum_{annulus} f_{l,c}(m . t) <= A_R [ l (rho + min(c, 2R)) + 2 Psi(l (2R - c)_+ / 2) ] <= A_R [ l (rho + c) + 2 + 2 log_+(l R) ],
    sum_{annulus} f_{l,c}(m . t)^2 <= A_R [ 4 l + (rho + c) l^2 ].

The edge factors are g_e = f_{l_e, 0} and the widened partner factors g~_f = f_{l_f, r_E}; the sums S_1^{(e)}, S_2^{(e)}, S~_1^{(f)}, S~_2^{(f)} of section 5 are these with t = t_e, c = 0 and t = t_f, c = r_E respectively.

Proof (the collaborator's form, #356, reproduced). For 0 < lambda <= l, {f_{l,c} >= lambda} = {|m . t| <= 2 / lambda + c}; since |m . t| <= |m| <= 2R on the annulus, the count is at most the strip count of Lemma 2 with u = min(2 / lambda + c, 2R), at most A_R (min(2 / lambda + c, 2R) + rho). The layer-cake formula gives sum f_{l,c} <= A_R int_0^l [rho + min(2 / lambda + c, 2R)] dlambda; the integrand is at most rho + min(c, 2R) + min(2 / lambda, (2R - c)_+), and int_0^l min(2 / lambda, K) dlambda = 2 Psi(l K / 2) for K > 0 (the constant branch K up to lambda = 2 / K, then the logarithm), which gives the first line, and the cruder second line follows from min(c, 2R) <= c and Psi(l R) <= 1 + log_+(l R). For the square, {f_{l,c}^2 >= mu} = {|m . t| <= 2 / sqrt(mu) + c} for 0 < mu <= l^2, so sum f_{l,c}^2 <= A_R int_0^{l^2} (2 / sqrt(mu) + c + rho) dmu = A_R [4 l + (c + rho) l^2], the integral of mu^{-1/2} converging at zero without a ball cap. Certified.

## 5. Theorem A, uniform in orientation

Let M >= 2 r_E. For every edge e of A and f of B, whatever the angle between their normals,

    sum_{(m, n) in P_A(M)} |a_m^{(e)}| |b_n^{(f)}| <= 32 N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| (1 + rho / (3 M)) sqrt(U_e U_f) / (D_A D_B D M),

    U_e = 4 l_e + rho l_e^2,   U_f = 4 l_f + (r_E + rho) l_f^2,

where a_m^{(e)} and b_n^{(f)} denote the e-term and the f-term of Lemma 1, and D = (2 pi)^2 / D_A is the cell area of Lambda_A^*. Summing over the edge pairs bounds the one-sided dropped mass: the rate is 1 / M for every orientation, and this is the complete angular contribution for shared and nearly parallel normals, strips and outside alike, including the coherent pairs at a fixed offset from a shared normal line (the collaborator's example, #351).

Proof. Fix the dyadic annulus R < |m| <= 2R with R = 2^j M. For (m, n) in P_A(M) with m in the annulus, |n| >= |m| - r_E >= R - r_E >= R / 2, and |n . t_f| >= |m . t_f| - r_E, so g_f(n . t_f) <= g~_f(m . t_f). By Lemma 1 the pair weight is at most |jump_e| |jump_f| g_e(m . t_e) g~_f(m . t_f) / (D_A D_B |m| |n|) <= 2 |jump_e| |jump_f| g_e(m . t_e) g~_f(m . t_f) / (D_A D_B R^2). Each m has at most N_E partners, so the annulus contributes at most (2 N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| / (D_A D_B R^2)) sum_{annulus} g_e(m . t_e) g~_f(m . t_f), and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality the sum is at most sqrt(S_2^{(e)}(R) S~_2^{(f)}(R)) <= A_R sqrt(U_e U_f) = 4 (2R + rho) sqrt(U_e U_f) / D by Lemma 3. The annulus therefore contributes at most 8 N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| (2R + rho) sqrt(U_e U_f) / (D_A D_B D R^2), and with R = 2^j M, sum_j (2R + rho) / R^2 = (2 / M) sum_j 2^{-j} + (rho / M^2) sum_j 4^{-j} = (4 / M)(1 + rho / (3 M)). Certified, given Lemmas 1 to 3.

Remark. The collaborator's independent derivation (#356) gives this bound with the same constant; the two derivations differ only in the order of the steps. For the half square against itself on the unit torus (D_A = D_B = 1, l = 1 / 2, jumps 1, rho = pi sqrt 2 in radians, N_E = 1, r_E small), one edge pair with a shared normal gives 32 (1 + rho / (3 M)) (2 + pi sqrt 2 / 4) / ((2 pi)^2 M) and more, against the coherent tail 1 / (18 pi^4 M_cycles) = 2 pi / (18 pi^4 M) of the example, which the bound must and does dominate; the constant is a certificate's, and the angular route of section 8 gives a smaller one under an asymptotic remainder.

## 6. Theorem B, separated normals

Let delta = |det(t_e, t_f)| = |sin psi| > 0 with psi the angle between the two normals (equivalently between the two tangents), and let M >= max(4 r_E / delta, 2 r_E). Then

    sum_{(m, n) in P_A(M)} |a_m^{(e)}| |b_n^{(f)}| <= (256 / 3) N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| (2 + rho / M) [Q_M + (4 / 3) log 2] / (delta D_A D_B D M^2),

    Q_M = rho (l_e + l_f) + r_E l_f + 4 + 2 log_+(l_e M) + 2 log_+(l_f M),

and for M >= rho the factor (2 + rho / M) / 3 may be replaced by 1. This includes every strip and off-strip contribution and has no remainder term.

Proof. Step 1, one tangent distance is large. For any m, max(|m . t_e|, |m . t_f|) >= delta |m| / 2. Otherwise m lies within the angle arcsin(delta / 2) of both normal lines, whose angle is psi (taken in (0, pi / 2] modulo the line symmetry), so psi <= 2 arcsin(delta / 2), that is sin(psi / 2) <= delta / 2 = sin(psi / 2) cos(psi / 2), impossible for 0 < psi < pi. Step 2, the case split (the collaborator's, #353). Let (m, n) be in P_A(M) with m in the annulus R < |m| <= 2R, R = 2^j M. If |m . t_e| >= delta |m| / 2 >= delta R / 2 then g_e(m . t_e) <= 4 / (delta R) and the product of the two tangent factors is at most (4 / (delta R)) g~_f(m . t_f). If instead |m . t_f| >= delta R / 2 then |n . t_f| >= delta R / 2 - r_E >= delta R / 4 by R >= 4 r_E / delta, so g_f(n . t_f) <= 8 / (delta R) and the product is at most (8 / (delta R)) g_e(m . t_e). In both cases g_e(m . t_e) g_f(n . t_f) <= (8 / (delta R)) [g_e(m . t_e) + g~_f(m . t_f)]: the sum of the two clipped factors replaces their product, and no double angular singularity is multiplied. Step 3, the annulus. With |n| >= R / 2 and at most N_E partners per m, the annulus contributes at most (N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| / (D_A D_B R (R / 2))) (8 / (delta R)) [S_1^{(e)}(R) + S~_1^{(f)}(R)] <= (16 N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| / (delta D_A D_B R^3)) A_R [rho l_e + 2 + 2 log_+(l_e R) + (rho + r_E) l_f + 2 + 2 log_+(l_f R)] = 64 N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| (2R + rho) Q_R / (delta D_A D_B D R^3) by Lemma 3, with Q_R as displayed at radius R. Step 4, the dyadic sum. With R = 2^j M, Q_R <= Q_M + 4 j log 2, and (2R + rho) / R^3 = 2 / (4^j M^2) + rho / (8^j M^3); using sum_j 4^{-j} = 4 / 3, sum_j j 4^{-j} = 4 / 9, sum_j 8^{-j} = 8 / 7 <= 4 / 3 and sum_j j 8^{-j} = 8 / 49 <= (4 / 3)(1 / 3), the sum is at most (4 / 3)(2 / M^2 + rho / M^3)[Q_M + (4 / 3) log 2], which gives the constant 64 (4 / 3) = 256 / 3 with the factor (2 + rho / M). Certified, given Lemmas 1 to 3.

Remark. The collaborator's independent derivation (#356) gives this bound with the same constant and the same Q_M; my earlier form (research log at a757bf3) had the cruder ball term 3 pi / 2 in place of 4 and required M >= rho and M >= 1 / l, which the refinement min(2 / lambda + c, 2R) in Lemma 3 removes.

## 7. The edge-pair partition and the composed certificate

[Superseded for the count by Theorem C of section 7b, which needs no partition; kept as the disc-shaped statement.] For each edge pair take the smaller of Theorem A and Theorem B (Theorem B only where its conditions hold; as delta shrinks the condition M >= 4 r_E / delta fails first and Theorem A takes over, so nearly parallel normals degrade continuously to the 1 / M rate and no separation is assumed, only paid for). Summing over edge pairs and adding the mirror set,

    dropped mass with beat in E <= sum_{e, f} [T_{ef}(M) + T'_{fe}(M)],

with T the chosen bound on P_A(M) and T' the same on P_B(M) with the roles of the lattices exchanged (D, rho, N_E replaced by the B-side quantities). The full-response certificate composes with the pair note's cuts: cutting both sources at M and the output at reach r_E, the filtered product changes by at most that dropped mass plus the output-tail allowance exp(-R_out^2 / 4) K_{Sigma / 2} ||a||_2 ||b||_2 of GAUSSIAN-LATTICE-PAIR.md (one set, no factor two), and the retained pairs are enumerated by its neighbour box, at most N_A(M) (1 + L_B) candidates with N_A(M) <= pi (M + rho)^2 / D; a fully explicit partner bound is N_E <= pi (r_E + rho_B)^2 / D' by Lemma 2 on the B side, or the shifted integer-box count of the pair note. Zero frequencies are separate area terms and enter neither tail.

What this buys. If every edge pair of the two materials has separated normals, the dropped mass is of order log M / M^2 and M of order (C_T log(1 / epsilon) / epsilon)^{1/2} meets a budget epsilon, so the retained candidates N_A(M) (1 + L_B) are of order (C_T / epsilon) log(1 / epsilon) (1 + L_B), against the general count of order epsilon^{-2} log(1 / epsilon) of the pair note. The reach r_E enters through c_0 (the term r_E l_f), through the condition M >= 4 r_E / delta, and through L_B; since the output tail forces R_out of order sqrt(log(1 / epsilon)), r_E grows like sqrt(log(1 / epsilon)) / sqrt(lambda_min(Sigma)), and the interaction with the angle threshold delta must be carried in any assembled price: for a footprint whose reach exceeds delta M / 4 the transverse theorem does not apply at that M. If some edge pair has parallel or nearly parallel normals, Theorem A governs that pair, M is of order C_U / epsilon, and the retained count is of order epsilon^{-2} again: the general bound is valid there but gives no improved list. An improved list for shared normals needs the coherent direction represented and enumerated on its own (the pairs near the common normal line, a one-dimensional family), which this note does not do; the two parallel gratings of the one-dimensional moire are the model problem.

## 7b. Theorem C, hyperbolic regions: the angle split removed (the collaborator's, #358; verified here)

The retained set need not be a disc, and once it is edge-adapted the orientation cases disappear. For a unit tangent t and a floor h > 0 define the hyperbolic weight

    X_t(m) = |m| max(h, |m . t|),

and retain the region X_t(m) <= T, the disc of radius T / h along the normal line thinning to radius T / u at tangential distance u: a hyperbolic cross adapted to the edge (classical; the count and tail laws are those of Dung, Temlyakov and Ullrich, eq. 2.16, no novelty claimed).

Lemma 4 (count and tail). On a lattice of cell area D and covering radius rho, with c = (1 + rho / h)^2 and T >= h^2,

    N_t(T) = #{m : X_t(m) <= T} <= (4 c T / D) [1 + (1 / 2) log(c T / h^2)],
    sum_{X_t(m) > T} X_t(m)^{-2} <= (4 c / (D T)) [3 + log(c T / h^2)].

Proof. Buffering: for m in the region with |m| >= h and m' within rho of m, |m'| <= |m| (1 + rho / h) and max(h, |m' . t|) <= max(h, |m . t|) (1 + rho / h), so X_t(m') <= c X_t(m) <= c T; for |m| < h, X_t(m) = h |m| < h^2 <= T and the point lies in the disc of radius h, whose buffered version lies in the region as well. The disjoint translated cells of the counted points therefore lie in {X_t <= c T}, whose area in tangent and normal coordinates is at most 2 (2 c T / h) h for |m . t| <= h plus int_h^{sqrt(c T)} 2 (2 c T / u) du = 2 c T log(c T / h^2) for the rest (the cross-section at tangential distance u is bounded by |normal| <= c T / u rather than the exact sqrt((c T / u)^2 - u^2), so this is an upper bound and not the area), that is at most 4 c T [1 + (1 / 2) log(c T / h^2)], and dividing by D gives the count. For the tail, sum_{X > T} X^{-2} = int_T^inf 2 [N_t(tau) - N_t(T)] tau^{-3} dtau <= int_T^inf 2 N_t(tau) tau^{-3} dtau <= (8 c / D) int_T^inf [1 + (1 / 2) log(c tau / h^2)] tau^{-2} dtau = (8 c / D)[1 / T + (log(c T / h^2) + 1) / (2 T)] = (4 c / (D T)) [3 + log(c T / h^2)]. Certified.

Lemma 5 (the clipped factors under the hyperbolic weight). If h >= 2 / l_e then g_e(s) = min(l_e, 2 / |s|) <= l_e h / max(h, |s|); if h >= r_E + 2 / l_f then g~_f(s) = min(l_f, 2 / (|s| - r_E)_+) <= l_f h / max(h, |s|). Proof. For |s| <= h both sides of the first are at most l_e and the right side equals it; for |s| > h, 2 / |s| <= l_e h / |s| is h >= 2 / l_e. For the second, |s| <= h as before; for |s| > h, 2 / (|s| - r_E) <= l_f h / |s| is |s| (l_f h - 2) >= l_f h r_E, which holds at |s| = h exactly when h >= r_E + 2 / l_f and then for all larger |s|. Certified.

Theorem C. Let h_e >= 2 / l_e, h_f >= r_E + 2 / l_f, T_e >= max(h_e^2, 4 r_E^2), T_f >= max(h_f^2, 4 r_E^2), and retain in Lambda_A^* the union of the two regions X_{t_e}(m) <= T_e and X_{t_f}(m) <= T_f (the second edge's region is taken in the outer lattice, the reach r_E having carried its factor to m). Outside the union, |m| > 2 r_E, so |n| >= |m| / 2, and the pair weight of the edge pair is, by Lemma 1, Lemma 5 and 2 g h <= g^2 + h^2,

    |a_m^{(e)}| |b_n^{(f)}| <= (2 |jump_e| |jump_f| / (D_A D_B)) g_e(m . t_e) g~_f(m . t_f) / |m|^2 <= (|jump_e| |jump_f| / (D_A D_B)) [ l_e^2 h_e^2 / X_{t_e}(m)^2 + l_f^2 h_f^2 / X_{t_f}(m)^2 ],

so the omitted edge-pair mass, with at most N_E partners per m and the two tails of Lemma 4, is at most

    (4 N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| / (D_A D_B D)) [ c_e l_e^2 h_e^2 (3 + log(c_e T_e / h_e^2)) / T_e + c_f l_f^2 h_f^2 (3 + log(c_f T_f / h_f^2)) / T_f ],

c_e = (1 + rho / h_e)^2, c_f = (1 + rho / h_f)^2, and the retained region has at most N_{t_e}(T_e) + N_{t_f}(T_f) points, of order T log T. No inverse sine and no angle enters. Certified, given Lemmas 1, 4 and 5. For a shared tangent the product form is sharper: g_e h~_f <= l_e l_f h^2 / max(h, |s|)^2 with one h >= max(2 / l_e, r_E + 2 / l_f), giving at most 8 c N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| l_e l_f h^2 (3 + log(c T / h^2)) / (D_A D_B D T) for T >= max(h^2, 4 r_E^2): the collaborator's repair of the off-strip coherent pairs of #351, which the hyperbolic region contains to the radius T / u at every offset u.

What changes. The retained set of the product in the outer lattice is the union over the edges of both materials of edge-adapted hyperbolic crosses, of size of order (E_A + E_B) T log T with E the edge counts, and the omitted mass is of order log T / T; at a budget epsilon and a fixed reach this is T of order (1 / epsilon) log(1 / epsilon) and a candidate count of order (1 / epsilon) log^2(1 / epsilon) times explicit constants, for every polygonal pair, aligned edges included (with the reach growing against the budget the count is section 7d's). Theorems A and B remain true and are now the disc-shaped special cases; the partition of section 7 is no longer needed for the count. The region is a count-map object, a lattice count in a region shaped by the material's own edges rather than by the footprint, and the footprint enters only through the reach r_E (in h_f and the conditions on T) and the partner count N_E. Enumeration, the collaborator's plan: cover each region by a central rectangle and dyadic tangent bands, and enumerate each rotated rectangle by integer-index rows, solving the other coordinate's interval, at O(T log T) candidates and O(T) row overhead at a fixed lattice and floor; the constants and their growth with r_E, N_E and h against the error budget are being assembled on their side.

## 7c. The common outer union (the collaborator's assembly, #360), challenged and found sound

Construction. Take one set S in Lambda_A^*, the union over every edge e of A of {X_{t_e} <= T_e} and every edge f of B of {X_{t_f} <= T_f} (the B-edge regions in A's frequency lattice, the reach having carried their factor to m), and evaluate the whole coefficients a_m b_n for every m in S and every n in Lambda_B^* with m + n in E. No separate cut on n: S is finite and each m has at most N_E partners, so the retained pairs number at most |S| N_E <= N_E [sum_e N_{t_e}(T_e) + sum_f N_{t_f}(T_f)], and the retained sum uses whole coefficients, so the pair note's Schur contract for its rounding is untouched by the edgewise envelopes, which enter only the omission bound.

Omission bound. For m not in S, m lies outside both regions of every edge pair, so by Lemma 1 (|a_m| |b_n| <= sum_{e, f} |a_m^{(e)}| |b_n^{(f)}|) and Theorem C's pointwise inequality the omitted mass is at most sum_{e, f} of the two tails of Lemma 4 times N_E |jump_e| |jump_f| / (D_A D_B); each edge's tail appears once per partner edge.

Checks made here. (i) Theorem C's pointwise inequality needs |m| > 2 r_E for m outside the union; this follows from the conditions on T and is not automatic: if |m| <= 2 r_E then X_t(m) <= 2 r_E max(h, 2 r_E) <= max(2 r_E h, 4 r_E^2) <= max(h^2, 4 r_E^2) <= T (the middle step by cases h >= 2 r_E and h < 2 r_E), so every m with |m| <= 2 r_E lies in every region, hence in S; contrapositively m outside S has |m| > 2 r_E. (ii) Zero modes: m = 0 has X = 0 and is in S; n = 0 needs m in E, |m| <= r_E, which is in S by (i); both are exact finite terms. (iii) The B side needs no mirror cut, since every pair with m in S is retained whatever n is; the earlier two-sided formulation is unnecessary here. (iv) The widened factor uses the reach r_E of E in every direction; for an anisotropic ellipse the bound |n . t_f| >= |m . t_f| - sup_{k in E} |k . t_f| holds with the support function of E in the direction t_f, which is smaller than r_E in the ellipse's short directions and would lower h_f there; a sharpening, not a correction. (v) Nesting in the reach. X_h(m) <= T holds exactly when |m| <= T / h and |m| |m . t| <= T, so the regions are nested whenever both T and T / h are nondecreasing; a set built for a larger output-frequency reach (a smaller footprint, larger r_E, larger h_f) contains the set for a smaller one under that rule and not otherwise. For a compact family of footprints lambda_- I <= Sigma <= lambda_+ I the collaborator's answer (#365) is simpler than nesting: take one uniform output radius and the worst reach b*, floor h* and partner bound N_E* over the family, and certify one common set S* directly, without asserting that optimized individual regions nest. None of these is an objection to the construction; (i) is a lemma the note now states, (iv) and (v) are for the price.

## 7d. The budget selector and the enumeration (the collaborator's, #361 and #362; verified here)

Selector. With one common floor h = max(2 r_E, r_E + 2 / l_min, rho) (the second term is what Lemma 5 needs for the widened factor; max(2 r_E, 2 / l_min, rho) would not imply it), c = (1 + rho / h)^2 <= 4, C_0 = 4 sum_{e, f} |jump_e| |jump_f| (l_e^2 + l_f^2) / (D_A D_B D), and one common threshold T = h^2 u with u >= 1 (so T >= h^2 and T >= 4 r_E^2 by h >= 2 r_E), the omission bound of section 7c reads at most K [3 + log(c u)] / u with K = C_0 N_E c, since each Theorem C term is c l^2 h^2 [3 + log(c u)] / (h^2 u). For a source budget eta, put v = max(1, K / eta), a = 3 + log c and u = 2 v [a + log(2 v)]; then with L = a + log(2 v) >= 1, a + log u = L + log L <= 2 L, and the omitted mass is at most K 2 L / (2 v L) = K / v <= eta. Explicit and sufficient; a constant source (all jumps zero) is a separate exact case.

Growth at a fixed footprint and fixed polygons (the collaborator's reading, reproduced): the output cut forces R_out of order sqrt(log(1 / epsilon)), so r_E, h and sqrt(N_E) are of that order, K is of order log(1 / epsilon), u of order epsilon^{-1} log^2(1 / epsilon), T of order epsilon^{-1} log^3, the union count of order epsilon^{-1} log^4, the neighbour box of order log, and the candidate arithmetic of order epsilon^{-1} log^5(1 / epsilon), plus whole-coefficient acquisition and precision. Deliberately conservative, and not a per-pixel speed claim.

Enumeration of one region {X_t <= T} without scanning its bounding box. Let J = ceil(log_2(sqrt(T) / h)) and a_j = 2^j h for j = 0, ..., J - 1. In the coordinates s = m . t (tangential) and w = m . n (normal), the region lies in the central rectangle |s| <= h, |w| <= T / h together with, for each j, the two rectangles a_j <= +-s <= min(2 a_j, sqrt(T)), |w| <= T / a_j (beyond |s| > sqrt(T) the region is empty, since |m| >= |s| gives X > T).     [ 4 T (J + 1) + 4 rho h 2^J + (20 - 16 2^{-J}) rho T / h + (8 J + 4) rho^2 ] / D.

Rows: with m = B (p, q) for the lattice basis B, alpha = e_1^T B^{-1} t and beta = e_1^T B^{-1} n, a rectangle's projection on the index p has length |alpha| w_r + |beta| z_r; enumerate those integer rows and, for each p, intersect the two linear coordinate constraints as intervals in q (a zero slope is a feasibility test). For the common union over the E_A + E_B edge regions, assign each point to the first region containing it by testing the preceding regions directly, O(E_A + E_B) membership checks per candidate and no global deduplication or sort. Coefficients are evaluated once per outer point and once per partner (repeated partner acquisition is priced conservatively); index magnitudes are at most sqrt(2) T / (h sigma_min(B)); precision and certified comparisons are charged separately. This establishes the enumeration, not a GPU speed. It is not a GPU result and not a general shader theorem.

## 7e. Indexing by classes, what it is and is not, and block sums along a shared direction

Lemma 6 (class indexing; not a compression). Let L be the linear map with Lambda_B^* = L Lambda_A^* (it exists for any two lattices). Every pair (m, n) with n = L m' has beat k = m + n = (I - L) m + L delta, delta = m + m' in Lambda_A^*, and the classes delta partition the pairs. (a) If I - L is invertible, each pair is recovered from its class and beat, m = (I - L)^{-1}(k - L delta), n = L (delta - m); the class beat sets (I - L) Lambda_A^* + L delta are shifted lattices, but they are disjoint only as labelled pair classes and not as sets of beats: for L = 2 I every class has beat set Z^2, and coincident beats from different classes sum coherently (the commensurate case, Lambda_B^* subset of Lambda_A^*). Beats are unique exactly when Lambda_A^* and Lambda_B^* meet only at zero, which is a different condition from the invertibility of I - L. (b) If I - L is singular its image need not be a discrete line lattice: for L = [[1/2, -sqrt 2], [0, 1]], invertible, (I - L) Z^2 = {(p / 2 + sqrt 2 q, 0)} is dense on a line; and for L = I every class k = delta contains the full convolution over m. (c) One coefficient evaluation per class point is one per pair: the indexing organizes the pairs and removes no evaluation; a class can be dropped under the region certificate only when every pair of it with beat in E lies outside the region, or its extra mass is bounded separately; and no fixed class list stays valid when L changes, without a family certificate. [After the collaborator's audit, #370; my earlier statement of a disjoint union of beat sets and of a "compression" is withdrawn.]

Block sums along a shared direction (proposal, the identities exact, the costs and certificate to be audited). Write x = (x_1, x_2) in coordinates where the shared direction is x_2 and both materials have the period 1 in x_2 (a direction in which the two lattices are commensurate; after a common affine change this is the case of interest, the incommensurate directions being where decomposition is unique and nothing sums). The partial coefficient functions A_d(x_1) = int_0^1 A(x_1, x_2) e^{-2 pi i d x_2} dx_2 = sum_p a_{(p, d)} e^{2 pi i p x_1} carry the whole spectrum of A on the line of tangential index d, and the same for B. For a beat k = (kappa, k_2) the sum over all pairs with tangential indices d and k_2 - d is

    sum_p a_{(p, d)} b_{(kappa - p, k_2 - d)} = the kappa-th Fourier coefficient of the product A_d(x_1) B_{k_2 - d}(x_1),

a one-dimensional closed form: for piecewise-constant sources on polygons, A_d is piecewise a sum of exponentials of linear functions of x_1 (the chord integrals between the polygon's edges), with breakpoints at the x_1-projections of the vertices, so the product's coefficient is an integral of products of exponentials over O(V_A + V_B) intervals, at a cost independent of the number of harmonics p summed. When the x_1 spacings are incommensurate as well, the sum over p has at most one term and the identity is the unique decomposition. The sum over d is truncated with the directional heat content (section 8 of the research log): the energy of A on the lines |d| > D is at most c* V_{t} / D with V_t the variation of A along the shared direction's normal, so the omitted blocks are certified by that law and the block count at a budget is of order sqrt(1 / epsilon) in |d| times the beats in E. This is the projection-slice structure of polygonal spectra (a line of the spectrum is the spectrum of a partial coefficient function), and it is where the coherent one-dimensional moire of two aligned materials is summed without enumerating its harmonics; the same-lattice case is the case where every direction is shared and the identity reproduces the refinement's exact coefficients by separation of variables. The spatial identity, the Gaussian integral over the cells of the arrangement, is the block sum of all pairs at once, cheap when the footprint holds few cells.

Preparation, restated. The pair coefficients a_m b_n do not depend on the pixel, so a certified list is prepared once per material pair: the pairs of the disc region are evaluated exactly (for two half squares at a source budget 1 / 512 about 5.5e5 of them, from schur-pair-certificate.mjs's printed 6.398e-3 at M = 128 scaling as 1 / M), sorted, and the absolute sum of every pair beyond the retained prefix is computed exactly, which is the certified omitted weight that section 7d's prefix formula asks for; per patch of pixels only the prefix enters the moment state, so the per-patch preparation is the prefix times O(n^2) and the per-pixel work is the moment recurrence. The compile-time count per material pair is the certified region's, the envelope's constants deciding it, and the block sums above reduce it along shared directions; the collaborator's point stands that this is the price of one construction and not a lower bound.

## 7g. The orthonormal Hermite state (the collaborator's, #375), audited here

State. Whiten the reference Gaussian gamma = N(mu_0, Sigma_ref) to N(0, I) by z = Sigma_ref^{-1/2}(x - mu_0), write F(z) for the product material A B in these coordinates (values in [0, 1]), and let h_alpha = He_alpha / sqrt(alpha!) be the orthonormal Hermite functions of L^2(gamma). The state is H_alpha = E_gamma[F h_alpha] for total degree |alpha| <= N, and Bessel gives sum_alpha |H_alpha|^2 <= E_gamma[F^2] <= 1.

Query. A pixel's Gaussian N(m, Q) in whitened coordinates, Q = I + D, has density ratio r = dN(m, Q) / dgamma, and its response is E_{N(m, Q)}[F] = E_gamma[F r] = sum_alpha H_alpha c_alpha with c_alpha = E_gamma[r h_alpha] = E_{N(m, Q)}[h_alpha], valid when r is in L^2(gamma), which holds for ||D|| <= r_0 < 1 (Q < 2 I). Checked here: the generating function sum_alpha t^alpha He_alpha(z) / alpha! = exp(t . z - |t|^2 / 2) gives E_{N(m, Q)} exp(t . z - |t|^2 / 2) = exp(t . m + t^T D t / 2), so the scaling c_alpha(sqrt w m, I + w D) = w^{|alpha| / 2} c_alpha(m, Q) is the substitution t -> sqrt w t; and S(w) = sum_alpha w^{|alpha|} |c_alpha|^2 = ||r_w||^2_{L^2(gamma)} for the ratio of N(sqrt w m, I + w D), which by direct Gaussian integration is det(I - w^2 D^2)^{-1/2} exp[w m^T (I - w D)^{-1} m] (verified in one dimension: the integral of N(m', 1 + w d)^2 / phi is (1 - w^2 d^2)^{-1/2} exp[w m^2 / (1 - w d)]). Hence, for w > 1 with w r_0 < 1, the truncation to total degree N has response error at most (sum_{|alpha| > N} |H_alpha|^2)^{1/2} (sum_{|alpha| > N} |c_alpha|^2)^{1/2} <= sqrt(S(w)) w^{-(N + 1) / 2}, uniformly S(w) <= (1 - w^2 r_0^2)^{-d / 2} exp[w L^2 / (1 - w r_0)] for ||m|| <= L, d = 2. Certified, given the collaborator's statement and these checks.

Errors in l2. An acquired state with l2 error eta changes the response by at most eta ||c||_2 = eta sqrt(S(1)), bounded for the query family with no monomial amplification e^{A_q}. Truncating the source spatially to |z| <= R changes the state by the coefficients of F 1_{|z| > R}, of l2 norm at most ||F 1_{out}||_{L^2(gamma)} <= sqrt(P_gamma(|z| > R)) = exp(-R^2 / 4) in two dimensions, simultaneously for every degree: the Hermite-tail obstacle of section 7f disappears when the error is measured collectively, which is the right measure for this state. The base oracle's per-coefficient errors eps_alpha combine as eta = (sum eps_alpha^2)^{1/2}.

Acquisition. From the arrangement (section 7f, now with the orthonormal weights): H_alpha = sum over the cells within radius R of v_C int_C h_alpha dgamma, Gaussian times Hermite polynomial over a polygon, all degrees up to N from one recurrence over each cell's edges, the cells sharing edges; the geometry and base-oracle costs of 7f apply and remain to be stated. From characters, for a single periodic material or any retained character list: since E_gamma[e^{i k . x} h_alpha] = e^{i k . mu_0} exp(-|k~|^2 / 2) (i k~)^alpha / sqrt(alpha!) with k~ = Sigma_ref^{1/2} k, the state is H_alpha = sum_k f^_k e^{i k . mu_0} exp(-|k~|^2 / 2) (i k~)^alpha / sqrt(alpha!), the count theorem's characters supplying it directly; a caution: a single character has l2 norm exactly one in the full Hermite basis (sum_alpha exp(-|k~|^2) |k~|^{2 |alpha|} / alpha! = 1), its mass sitting at degrees near |k~|^2, so omitting characters is harmless only for the truncated state, whose low-degree coefficients of an omitted character are exp(-|k~|^2 / 2) |k~|^{|alpha|} / sqrt(alpha!), small when |k~|^2 is well above N; the ellipse of the count theorem must therefore be taken at a reach that grows with sqrt N, a bound to be written.

Prior art and attribution (the collaborator's finding, #376). The Gaussian density-ratio norm and the weighted norm S(w) above are Theorems 6.1 and 7.1 (equation 11) of Jae Wan Shim, Parameter-Space Heat Flow, Gaussian Density Ratios, and Sharp Hermite Truncation Rates (July 2026 preprint, arXiv 2607.07712), with t^2 = w, and Theorem 8.1 there gives the sharp root rate sqrt(||Q - I||); the contribution here is the bounded-material query application, the collective spatial-truncation bookkeeping by Bessel, and the cost distinctions, not the identity. The collaborator documents the application in GAUSSIAN-QUERY-STATE.md; its numerical contract is preferred within an admitted patch without a claim that it dominates the finite-band monomial cache everywhere.

Why the admission is a band and not a one-sided range (the collaborator's argument, #376). For a periodic stripe F = A^2 = A with mu_0 inside a stripe, the heat response f(lambda) tends to one with an exponentially small correction as lambda tends to zero, so it is not analytic at zero, and its Taylor series in the covariance at lambda_0 has radius at most lambda_0; the full-source Hermite state at a reference Sigma_ref reproduces that series for Delta mu = 0 and cannot converge for a covariance increment beyond the reference, which is what ||D|| <= r_0 < 1 says. The finite-band cache avoids this by being an entire function of the covariance. Consequently a smaller reference footprint worsens both convergence and conditioning, and the natural direction is a larger reference with an admitted local patch [my suggestion of a smaller reference in #374 is withdrawn].

Query arithmetic (the collaborator's, #376, independently checked on their side). The normalized coefficients obey c_{alpha + e_i} = [m_i c_alpha + sum_j D_ij sqrt(alpha_j) c_{alpha - e_j}] / sqrt(alpha_i + 1), c_0 = 1, so a canonical predecessor per target computes all K_N = binom(N + d, d) coefficients in O(d K_N) and the contraction costs O(K_N); with state l2 error eta_H and coefficient l2 error eta_c the error is at most sqrt(S(1)) eta_H + eta_c + eta_H eta_c plus the summation error, and finite-precision accuracy of the recurrence still needs its certificate. On a fixed admitted patch the degree is N = O(log(1 / epsilon)), so state and query are O(log^2(1 / epsilon)) in two dimensions, with no claim about acquisition.

The all-order edge recurrence (the collaborator's, #380; signs and recurrence derived independently here). ## 7h. Broader acquisition: what else feeds the same state, and what does not (proposal for audit)

The final query representation accepts any fixed bounded F; the question (the collaborator's, #387) is a cheap enough interface from material programs whose parts are not piecewise constant on polygons. Four extensions of the polygonal boundary oracle of GAUSSIAN-QUERY-STATE.md section 8, each with a validity and cost form and none with a constant, and two exclusions.

(a) Piecewise polynomial on polygonal cells [corrected after #390]. Let F = sum_c p_c 1_{P_c} with p_c of degree at most d. Do not integrate by parts with the polynomial weight: the identity h_beta gamma = -(1 / sqrt(beta_i)) partial_i(h_{beta - e_i} gamma) needs beta_i > 0 and the recursion can reach beta = 0 before the polynomial is constant (already H_0 with q = z_1). Instead expand first: by the multiplication identity z_i h_beta = sqrt(beta_i + 1) h_{beta + e_i} + sqrt(beta_i) h_{beta - e_i}, a polynomial q of degree d times h_alpha is a combination of h_{beta} with |beta| <= |alpha| + d, so int_{P_c} p_c h_alpha gamma = sum_beta C_{c, alpha, beta} M_{c, beta} with M_{c, beta} = int_{P_c} h_beta gamma the unweighted cell moments through degree N + d, which the collaborator's section 8 oracle provides (cell moments assembled from the edge recurrence and the Phi_2 seed at degree N + d). The complete conservative construction (the collaborator's, #391, independently audited on their side; verified here). For each supplied cell c compute the unweighted moment vector M^c_beta = int_{P_c} h_beta gamma through degree N + d, including H_0 with its Phi_2 seed, by the section 8 oracle. Let X_i be the multiplication-by-z_i operator on moment vectors, (X_i v)_alpha = sqrt(alpha_i + 1) v_{alpha + e_i} + sqrt(alpha_i) v_{alpha - e_i}, symmetric and sparse, X_1 and X_2 commuting. Since p_c(z) h_alpha(z) = sum_beta [p_c(X)]_{beta alpha} h_beta(z) by the multiplication identity applied monomial by monomial, int_{P_c} p_c h_alpha gamma = [p_c(X_1, X_2) M^c]_alpha, and the state is sum_c [p_c(X_1, X_2) M^c]_alpha for |alpha| <= N; the padding to degree N + d covers the beta = 0 case automatically. Cost, for dense supplied bivariate monomial coefficients: nested Horner in two variables needs O((d + 1)^2) applications of the sparse operators, each O((N + d + 1)^2), so a safe total is O([E_inc + C (d + 1)^2] (N + d + 1)^2) plus O(E_inc) seed special-function calls, the geometry, the input and basis conversion and the precision, with C the supplied cells and E_inc their boundary incidences (including interfaces with a continuous value but different polynomial pieces); cells can be streamed; for fixed d and C = O(E_inc) the scaling of the piecewise-constant oracle is recovered with a degree-dependent constant. A sufficient algorithm and not a lower bound against better contractions; coefficient magnitudes and cancellation enter the numerical budget. Validity is that of piecewise-constant sources with the polynomial pieces supplied.

(b) Curved count boundaries (conic arcs, the circles material). The divergence identity int_C h_{alpha + e_i} gamma = -(1 / sqrt(alpha_i + 1)) oint_{partial C} h_alpha gamma n_i ds holds for every piecewise-smooth boundary, so a curved arc contributes a one-dimensional integral of h_alpha gamma n_i along it; along an arc the Gaussian is not a Gaussian in the parameter, so the arc needs a quadrature edge oracle instead of the closed recurrence: Gauss-Legendre panels on the arc's parameter (the kernel's disc panels are this oracle without a certificate), with the standard panel error bound from the derivatives of the integrand, which is entire and bounded through Cramer's bound on h_alpha gamma times the arc's parametrization derivatives, polynomial in the curvature and the degree. Validity: the arc explicit and its parametrization's derivatives bounded; cost: panels per arc times nodes times the O(K_N) Hermite evaluations per node, with the certificate's derivative constants to be written; the same for any explicit smooth boundary.

(c) Smooth amplitude factors on a fixed mask. For F = g F' with F' one of the above and g smooth on the window, replace g by its Taylor polynomial T_d g at mu_0 and use (a); the state error is collective, ||(g - T_d g) F'||_{L^2(gamma)} <= sup_window |g - T_d g| plus the Gaussian tail outside the window, by Bessel, for every degree at once, once the Taylor remainder is certified. Validity: a derivative bound on g over the window; cost: (a) at degree N + d. Lighting enters here. A smooth warp of a discontinuous mask does not: it moves the mask's boundaries, so it changes the arrangement and is not an amplitude factor; its affine part is a change of the lattices and its remainder is the projective correction's business, and the warped boundaries must be supplied as curves for (b) [distinction kept after #390].

(d) Gabor atoms times polygonal masks [corrected after #390: the standard recurrence does not hold verbatim under a Gaussian factor]. Let the atom be g(z) = exp(-z^T A z / 2 + i k . z) in whitened coordinates, A symmetric with I + A positive definite (the admitted covariance), and let I^g_alpha = int_edge h_alpha gamma g ds in the ORIGINAL Hermite basis of gamma, which is the basis of the state. Exclusions.
