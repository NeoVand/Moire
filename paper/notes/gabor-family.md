# Authored Gabor fields: a finite-source statement with four costs separated

A draft for the collaborator's audit, 2026-09-06 (bridge #184, #186, #189). The source is a finite authored list of untruncated Gabor atoms, not a Poisson process; the arithmetic model is exact reals with the Gaussian density, the error function and the complex exponential as primitives; precision is a separate obligation. Nothing here is a GPU cost or a frame budget. The linear closure is Lagae, Lefebvre, Drettakis and Dutre's (SIGGRAPH 2009, section 5.2), stated here with anisotropic envelopes so that it is exact; the rest is this note's, with novelty unestablished.

## 1. The source

A material coordinate plane with a lattice of period P in each axis. An authored list of n atoms per period cell, repeated periodically: atom i has amplitude c_i (a real number, |c_i| <= W), centre x_i, envelope precision Lambda_i (symmetric positive definite, with eigenvalues at least lambda_min), frequency f_i (a vector), phase phi_i. The field is

    N(x) = sum over cells and atoms of c_i exp(-(1 / 2)(x - x_i)^T Lambda_i (x - x_i)) cos(2 pi f_i . (x - x_i) + phi_i),

the sum over the periodic images of every atom. Each real atom is the real part of a complex Gaussian exponential, (1 / 2)[E_i(x) + conj E_i(x)] with E_i(x) = c_i exp(i phi_i) exp(-(1 / 2)(x - x_i)^T Lambda_i (x - x_i) + 2 pi i f_i . (x - x_i)). So N is a finite sum of members of the complex-Gaussian algebra of the research note's section 12, periodized; there is no indexing map, no hash and no random count: the translation action is trivial, and the only source parameters are the authored ones. Untruncated atoms overlap every cell; the periodic images beyond the nearest are exponentially small, and their omission is charged in section 4.

The pixel footprint is Gaussian with mean mu and covariance Sigma in material coordinates, exact for an affine map from the screen; for a perspective map the footprint is not Gaussian in material coordinates and the depth conditioning of the kernel (exact on lines transverse to the depth direction, a one-dimensional quadrature along it) is the map-side extension, stated separately from everything below.

## 2. Linear closure, exact, and its two branches

For a complex Gaussian exponential E_i and X ~ N(mu, Sigma), completing the square gives the closed form

    E E_i(X) = c_i exp(i phi_i) det(I + Sigma Lambda_i)^(-1/2) exp(-(1 / 2)(mu - x_i)^T (Sigma + Lambda_i^-1)^-1 (mu - x_i)) exp(2 pi i f_i . (m_i - x_i) - 2 pi^2 f_i^T S_i f_i),

with S_i = (Sigma^-1 + Lambda_i)^-1 and m_i = S_i (Sigma^-1 mu + Lambda_i x_i): the product of the footprint and the envelope is a Gaussian with precision Sigma^-1 + Lambda_i and mean m_i, and the harmonic's expectation under it is the last factor. This is the collaborator's section 5.2 in spatial form with the anisotropic envelope kept (their eqs. 19 to 23 approximate S_i by an isotropic matrix by choice); the filtered atom is again a complex Gaussian exponential in mu, so the filtered field is in the algebra. The oscillatory factor exp(-2 pi^2 f_i^T S_i f_i) is not the pure multiplier exp(-2 pi^2 f_i^T Sigma f_i) of a plane wave: S_i <= min(Sigma, Lambda_i^-1) in the matrix order, so as the footprint grows the factor saturates at exp(-2 pi^2 f_i^T Lambda_i^-1 f_i), the atom's own low-frequency content, which is small when the frequency exceeds the envelope's bandwidth and never zero (#186). The far field depends on which of two source contracts is meant, and they are kept distinct (#196). For a finite nonperiodic list the filtered value at a fixed centre decays algebraically, as the footprint-weighted sum of the atoms' integrals I_i = c_i cos(phi_i) (2 pi) det(Lambda_i)^(-1/2) exp(-2 pi^2 f_i^T Lambda_i^-1 f_i), the sigma^-2 factor times the weight sums the footprint reaches (the collaborator's #186). For the periodic source of this note, with Sigma = t Sigma_0 and t growing, the filtered field tends to the mean (1 / P^2) sum over the cell's atoms of I_i, and if that mean is exactly zero the nonzero lattice harmonics decay exponentially in t; signed weights alone do not make the mean zero when widths or carriers differ, so a zero mean is an authoring constraint to be imposed and then certified from the I_i, not assumed.

Spatial branch (the atom sum). The exact value is the real part of the sum of the closed forms over all atoms and images. Its certificate is the bound per atom, |E E_i(X)| <= |c_i| det(I + Sigma Lambda_i)^(-1/2) exp(-(1 / 2) d_i^2) with d_i the Mahalanobis distance of x_i from mu under Sigma + Lambda_i^-1, so atoms and images beyond a radius R in that metric contribute at most the sum of those bounds. That sum is a shifted-lattice Gaussian sum and is not bounded by the lattice density times the continuum tail integral (one atom just outside the radius contributes a finite term while the continuum integral per cell vanishes as the period grows, #196); the bound is by cell covering with inflation: each period cell beyond the radius contributes at most n W times the maximum of the Gaussian factor over the cell, and the sum over those cells is at most the continuum integral over the region inflated by a cell diagonal, divided by the cell area, plus the boundary ring of cells, explicit and deterministic. Work: the number of atoms and images within the radius, at most n times the number of period cells the dilated footprint reaches, each one closed form. This is the count theorem's spatial branch with an exact cell integrator, the atom sum, and no wedge or conic primitive.

Spectral branch (the harmonics). N is P-periodic and bounded (|N| <= n W times the image sum, a finite constant), so it is a material of the count theorem's class on the torus of period P, with the exact Fourier coefficient at the harmonic k = 2 pi n / P given by P^-2 times the finite sum over the cell's n atoms of their transforms (the periodization turns the continuous transform into the coefficient with the factor 1 / P^2, #196): each atom's transform is the sum of two Gaussians in frequency, c_i (pi / sqrt(det Lambda_i)) exp(-(1 / 2)(k -+ 2 pi f_i)^T Lambda_i^-1 (k -+ 2 pi f_i)) times the phase exp(-i k . x_i) exp(+- i phi_i), so the coefficient oracle costs 2 n Gaussian evaluations per harmonic and needs no table. The density lemma then certifies the truncation to the ellipse k^T Sigma k <= c with the tail (Delta / 2) sqrt(sum over the omitted harmonics of exp(-k^T Sigma k)), Delta the field's range, needing no source tail (#183); the retention level is the count theorem's c(eps, kappa). Work: the retained harmonic count of the count theorem times 2 n.

Statement (linear). For an authored periodic Gabor field, the filtered value to tolerance eps is computable exactly on either branch with the count theorem's term counts, the material work being n closed forms per atom term or 2 n Gaussians per harmonic, and the crossover and the anisotropic branch as in the count theorem; the spatial branch is the paper's method made exact, the spectral branch is new to this family, and the far field is the spectral branch with the atoms' integrals as its zero mode. The obligations of the count theorem (all scales, precision, the retention level's conditioning) carry over unchanged.

## 3. Polynomial composition

The product of two real atoms is a sum of two complex Gaussian exponentials at the sum and difference frequencies (#186), each with envelope precision Lambda_i + Lambda_j, centre the precision-weighted mean (Lambda_i + Lambda_j)^-1 (Lambda_i x_i + Lambda_j x_j), frequency f_i +- f_j, and amplitude c_i c_j / 2 times the cross term exp(-(1 / 2)(x_i - x_j)^T (Lambda_i^-1 + Lambda_j^-1)^-1 (x_i - x_j)). A polynomial of degree K in a finite collection of such fields is therefore again a finite sum of complex Gaussian exponentials, filtered exactly by section 2 term by term. The budget: pairs whose cross term exceeds the tolerance's share, at most n^2 times the number of cells within envelope reach for degree two, and n^K times reach^(K - 1) for degree K, an explicit upper bound and not an intrinsic cost; the certificate is the sum of the dropped terms' amplitude bounds. The difference frequency is the beat, so the moire between two authored Gabor fields is exact per pair, and its far field is the spectral branch of the product field, whose harmonics carry the difference frequencies. The collaborator's compression of the squared field (#191, #192), stated for a finite nonperiodic authored list with a positive-semidefinite affine footprint and not for the periodic images of this note, which would need a certified image selection (#196), replaces the pairs inside a cluster by feature sums: for J atoms sharing an envelope precision A under a footprint X = mu + D Z with Z standard in r dimensions, C = D (I + 2 D^T A D)^-1 D^T = L L^T, the normalized atom Gram is the coherent-state kernel in the phase-space coordinates b_j = L^T (A d_j + i nu_j) relative to a reference centre and carrier (a common carrier is absorbed exactly, so only relative centres and frequencies enter), truncating the coherent-state features to total degree p gives r_p = binom(p + r, r) features (r = 2 for a rank-two footprint even in a three-dimensional material), the truncation is an orthogonal projection in the footprint-weighted source space so 0 <= V - V_p <= S^2 T_p(R^2) with S the sum of the atoms' root energies, R the phase-space radius of the cluster and T_p the Poisson tail, a sufficient degree being p + 1 >= max(2 e R^2, log_2(S^2 / eps)), and the cost is O(J r_p) with no explicit pairs. Clusters compose with the cross term of this section: omitted cross-cluster interactions contribute at most 2 sum over cluster pairs of S_C S_D exp(-d_CD^2 / 2), d_CD the phase-space gap between the clusters' balls, since the normalized Gram magnitude is exp(-|z_i - z_j|^2 / 2), and the total error is two-sided (independent cluster energies are not a lower bound); a real field needs the conjugate atoms, and (Re u)^2 keeps the sum-frequency lobe, which cannot be dropped without that certificate. The principle of aggregating Gaussian pair sums into diameter-controlled feature sums is prior art (Joshi et al., kernel distance, section 4.2). Open in that route: atoms of different envelope precisions in one product (a product of two noises of different bandwidths), and any response beyond quadratic. This note's enumeration is the fallback with the explicit budget.

## 4. Truncation and images

The paper truncates each atom where its envelope reaches 5 percent of its peak. A truncated atom is the untruncated atom times the indicator of an ellipse, which leaves the algebra; the filtered truncated atom equals the closed form of section 2 minus the atom's mass outside the ellipse under the footprint, bounded by |c_i| det(I + Sigma Lambda_i)^(-1/2) times the Gaussian mass of the product measure outside the ellipse, computable per atom and summed as an explicit source-modification budget; it is not a uniform 5 percent. This note takes the source as untruncated and charges instead the periodic images: an atom's image at lattice distance L P contributes at most |c_i| exp(-(1 / 2) lambda_min (L P - r)^2) at any point within distance r of the atom, so the images beyond the nearest are bounded by a geometric series in exp(-lambda_min P^2 / 2), explicit, and the field's range Delta of section 2 is computed with that bound.

## 5. Thresholds

A threshold 1{N > t} is not in the algebra and its boundary is transcendental (#186); it is the count theorem's threshold node on the torus of period P, in three parts with separate contracts. Near field: the jet of N at the pixel is closed form (derivatives of Gaussians times cosines), and the third derivatives are bounded on any ball by explicit sums over the atoms in reach of |c_i| (2 pi |f_i| + sqrt(||Lambda_i||) rho)^3 times envelope factors, so the coverage enclosure of the theory ledger (lower and upper coverage from one cubic inequality per angular arc) applies with an explicit remainder constant and returns a certified interval; its root isolation and endpoint masses are the open obligations already recorded there. Far field: the indicator is a bounded material on the torus, so the spectral branch applies with the density lemma's certificate and no source tail; its material work is the indicator's Fourier coefficients on the retained ellipse, which are boundary integrals along the level curves of N by the divergence theorem, traced once at compile time with a certified root-tracing method (the level set of a nonconstant real-analytic function has measure zero, so assumption (a) of the shared-phase statement holds at every pixel where N is not identically t on the leaf, and here the leaf is the whole plane), an explicit compile-time cost per coefficient and the one place where the region's transcendence is paid. Transition band: the line method of the mask node, exact roots by bracketing with the closed-form Hermite bounds and Gauss-Legendre across, as a numerical method with a work cap and a residual interval, not a closed form. What is not claimed: a bound on the number of level-curve components in a footprint (the analogue of winding), which is the transition band's cost and is a property of the authored list.

## 6. What this says for the product

An authored Gabor field is a material family for which every part of the theory is stated at once for a finite source, after the shared-carrier graphs and the carry-state noises: exact linear filtering on both branches with the count theorem's costs and certificates, exact polynomial composition with an enumeration budget, an explicit truncation budget, and thresholds as the theorem's threshold node with the transcendence paid once at compile time. It is also a family the industry already uses. The reasons to put it before hashed noise in the node set are that its source has no indexing map, so composition has no difference classes and no carry state, and that its far field has an exact spectral branch. Against it: the pair budget grows like n^K, the near-field work is n atoms per cell reached, and the threshold's transition band has no proved bound. Open: the compression of the pair budget (the collaborator's #189), the component count of thresholds, precision, and the map-side extension to perspective.
