# Related work and an audit of the anti-aliasing claim

Written 2026-09-04, after reading the papers below against what `fjet.mjs` does. The question this note answers is the one the author asked: are we comparing against the state of the art, and do we have something nobody has done, with a serious application. The short answer is yes on both counts, with four weaknesses that the benchmark note must state and that Stage 2 must fix.

## The lineage the benchmark belongs to

Norton, Rockwood and Skolmoski (SIGGRAPH 1982, "Clamping: a method of antialiasing textured surfaces by bandwidth limiting in object space") filtered a periodic texture by dropping the terms of its Fourier series past the pixel's Nyquist rate. That is the multiplier theorem with a hard cut instead of the window's transform, for one count and no composition. Everything since, in the procedural line, has been about how to filter a shader that is not a Fourier series.

Dorn, Barnes, Lawrence and Weimer (CGF 2015, "Towards automatic band-limited procedural shaders") compiled closed-form integral approximations per node of the shader's expression tree, with a per-node sigma set by simple rules and a genetic search over the approximations. Yang and Barnes (CGF 2018, "Approximate program smoothing using mean-variance statistics, with application to procedural shader bandlimiting") replaced the per-node integrals by mean and variance propagation: every intermediate is a random variable with two moments, each primitive has rules for its smoothed mean and variance (a table of Gaussian-smoothed univariate functions, an adaptive Gaussian rule exact to second order in sigma for analytic functions, Monte Carlo and compact kernels as fallbacks), and a genetic search picks the rule per node. The authors state the assumption: intermediate distributions are Gaussian. The bimodal distribution of fract near its jump is handled by truncating the kernel at the discontinuity, which they describe as a special case. Their protocol is the one `fjet-yb.mjs` reproduces: 480 by 320, Gaussian jitter of sigma 0.5 pixel, 1000 samples per pixel for the truth, L2 error after clamping. Their best errors on the shaders we run are 0.035 (circles) to 0.071 (checkerboard with ripples); our automatic frames sit at the noise floor of the same protocol, 0.0045 to 0.0075.

Li, Wang, Huo, Zheng, Hua and Bao (CGF 2020, "Automatic band-limited approximation of shaders using mean-variance statistics in clamped domain", Pacific Graphics) kept the framework and added rules that respect the domain and range of composed functions (kernels renormalised on the clamped domain, a Gaussian fit of the output density, second-order Taylor), plus textures and normal maps through a Toksvig-like normal distribution. Their table 2 measures PSNR against a box-supersampled truth on the same fifteen shaders: unfiltered 17.1 dB on average, Yang and Barnes 24.8, theirs 28.0, and the ideal Gaussian filter 30.3. So the best mean-variance method is still 2 to 3 dB short of the Gaussian pixel it approximates, and the Gaussian pixel is what we compute. They run at 5700 frames a second on a teapot, the same as LEAN and the unfiltered shader.

Yang, Barnes, Adams and Finkelstein (TOG 2022, "A-delta: autodiff for discontinuous programs, applied to shaders") and Belhe, Mehta, Chang, Georgiev, Gharbi, Ramamoorthi and Li (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025, "Automatic sampling for discontinuities in differentiable shaders") are the same group's later direction: gradients of a prefiltered shader across its discontinuities, by sampling the boundaries. A-delta approximates the prefiltered gradient over a box kernel along one sampling axis; Belhe et al. transform the program into a piecewise-constant part and boundary terms and sample both.

## Spectral prefiltering of features

Barron et al. (ICCV 2021, mip-NeRF) introduced the integrated positional encoding: the expectation of sin(k x) under a Gaussian is sin(k mu) exp(-k^2 sigma^2 / 2). That is the first-order multiplier theorem for a pure sinusoid of one count. The network after the features is trained on filtered features; it is not filtered. Yaldiz, Mehta, Raghavan, Meuleman, Li and Ramamoorthi (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025, "Spectral prefiltering of neural fields") scale every Fourier feature by the filter's frequency response, exactly for Gaussian, box and Lanczos filters, and train the MLP on single-sample Monte Carlo estimates of the filtered signal so that the nonlinearity's effect is learned. No composition of periodic functions, no counts, no discontinuities, and no citation of the procedural line above.

## Closed forms for one structure at a time

Each of these is one row of the table in `paper/notes/antialiasing.md`: the expectation of one specific shader under the pixel's Gaussian, worked out for that shader. Lagae and Drettakis (2011) filter solid Gabor noise exactly by filtering each kernel's slice. Tricard et al. (2019, procedural phasor noise) apply a periodic profile to a smooth phase field, which in our words is a picture on a count, and filter the profile. Heitz, Nowrouzezahrai, Poulin and Neyret (2013) filter colour-mapped textures by applying the colour map to a Gaussian model of the texture, a one-dimensional step of a sum; Heitz et al. (2014) do the same for non-linear transfer functions with masking. Han, Sun, Ramamoorthi and Grinspun (2007) filter normal maps as a spherical convolution of the normal distribution with the BRDF, in spherical harmonics or von Mises-Fisher lobes: the shaded picture on the sphere, for the normal only. LEAN (Olano and Baker 2010), LEADR (Dupuy et al. 2013), Toksvig (2005) and Kaplanyan et al. (2016) keep moments of the normal. Manson and Schaefer (2013) rasterise curves exactly under polynomial filters by boundary integrals: exact coverage of edges, no periodic structure, no perspective. Bruneton and Neyret (TVCG 2012, "A survey of nonlinear prefiltering methods for efficient and accurate surface shading") frame the whole problem as prefiltering a nonlinear function of filtered inputs and survey the per-structure answers. Quilez's band-limiting article box-filters a sine in closed form and states the obstacle that all of these work around: the integral of a composition is not the composition of the integrals.

## What is ours

Nothing in the list computes the Gaussian-filtered value of a general procedural shader by decomposing it. Ours does: the shader is traced once into counts and pictures (the one rule: a non-smooth primitive makes the smooth part of its argument a count), the pixel's covariance is pushed through every count to second order, the recipes whose combined rate is slow are enumerated with their stations and folds, and the counts that are slow across the pixel are integrated by quadrature with their jumps located. The ingredients that the literature does not have, each of which was forced by a wrong pixel:

- the count algebra with fields (a count shifted by a picture on other counts) and compositions, so that sin(floor(s) + floor(t)) or a specular lobe over a rippled normal is one term and not a special case;
- stations between counts (a harmonic of one count cancelling a harmonic of another through the perspective), which mean-variance propagation cannot see because it keeps two moments per intermediate; these are exactly where its error is;
- the second-order pushforward, the Gaussian integral of a quadratic phase in closed form, which is what makes a fold (a count stationary inside the pixel) a formula rather than a search;
- the step of a sum: a non-smooth function of a constant plus pictures on some counts plus pictures with fields over them, whose transform is exact between the located roots and jumps, tabulated once and read at evaluation;
- the automatic compiler that does all of this from the shader's source, and reaches the protocol's noise floor on every shader of the public benchmark it traces.

The unifying statement, which the benchmark note should make, is that mean-variance propagation is a two-moment approximation of the desert limit of the count algebra. When a term's counts have no station, the pixel sees the pictures through the uniform measure on their torus, and the moment rules are exact when that measure's pushforward through the sum of pictures is Gaussian, which the central limit theorem grants for many independent pictures and denies for one (a sign of a constant plus a sine sees the arcsine law, not a Gaussian). Where a station exists the torus measure is not the right one at all, and that is where their error concentrates. The theory says which case a term is in before anything is computed.

## Where we could be fooling ourselves

1. The target. The benchmark's truth is the Gaussian-filtered image at sigma 0.5 pixel, and our exactness is exactness for that pixel. Li et al. measure against a box-supersampled truth and the ideal Gaussian itself is 2 to 3 dB short of it. A box pixel is a different multiplier (a sinc) and the second-order pushforward through curved counts is no longer a closed form for it, though the near field's quadrature would work unchanged. The claim to make is "the Gaussian pixel, exactly", not "the pixel".
2. Breadth. Seven shaders on one plane and one camera. Their set has twenty-one, including bricks with noise. Procedural noise (Perlin, Gabor) is a picture on a large torus for which the tables would be large; we have not run it. The bumps variants (spheres) have not been run.
3. Speed. The compiler costs 2 to 40 milliseconds a pixel on one core, 100 to 1000 times the unfiltered shader, against their 1 to 4 times. The GPU path (the spectrum as the table, measured in `tables.mjs` to need 175 terms a pixel on average) is a design with one measurement, not a demonstration.
4. Tuned thresholds. The rule is one, but the evaluator decides local against spectral by sigma 0.02, the parallel rule by sigma 0.03 and sine 0.26, the fold growth cap 2, the cut 1e-4, the line by 24 periods. Each value was set by measurement on these shaders. The theory says what each threshold trades, not what it should be.
5. One probe pixel above 1e-3 remains away from the horizon row (the rippled quadratic sine's on-axis pixel at 1.4e-3, down from 4.6e-3 once fields were treated as jets across the pixel), the row y = 5 carries 1e-3 from the third-order phase, and the step-of-a-sum tables truncate the Phi harmonics at 24, which drops the far stations of sin(n) over the integers (the convergents 333/53 and beyond of 2 pi) at the 1e-4 level.

## What the reading suggests

- Exact prefiltered gradients. The jets are second-order automatic differentiation already; the compiler's expectation is differentiable in the shader's parameters. That is what A-delta and Belhe et al. approximate by sampling boundaries.
- Spectral prefiltering of neural fields through the first nonlinearity, exactly, by the step of a sum: a ReLU of a sum of sinusoids of counts is a picture on their torus. With many features the torus is large and the desert limit returns the Gaussian statistics that Yaldiz et al. learn.
- The comparison table to add: Yang and Barnes's zigzag with ripples 0.045, fire with bumps 0.037, colour circles with bumps 0.039, against our frames on the same protocol; Li et al.'s PSNRs with the box-truth caveat stated.
