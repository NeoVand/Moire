# The threshold oracle: a contract page

The specification a probe or an implementation of a thresholded Gabor intensity's pixel mean has to meet, fixed before anything is run. Assembled from bridge #269 to #273 and the collaborator's GABOR-WEIGHTED-MOMENTS.md, GABOR-THRESHOLD-MOMENTS.md and PROJECTIVE-DENSITY.md. Every item names its status: proved on the collaborator's side, checked here numerically, or open.

## 1. Source and material

A finite authored Gabor field with a common envelope: F(x) = sum_{j = 1}^J a_j exp(-(x - c_j)^T A (x - c_j) / 2) exp(i omega_j . x) with A positive definite, real centres c_j and frequencies omega_j, complex amplitudes a_j. The intensity is h = |F|^2 with the global range 0 <= h <= W, W = (sum_j |a_j|)^2. The material is the threshold 1{h >= tau} with tau in (0, W); endpoint thresholds, a constant field and known-support cases are outside this page and need separate treatment.

## 2. Footprint, state, and the two targets

The footprint is the pixel's Gaussian in whitened screen coordinates, Z standard Gaussian in r = 2 dimensions; the state is X = mu + D Z on the plane (the shared planar chart; nothing nonlinear in Z is a separate count). Two targets:

- the affine surrogate's response, I_A(tau) = P(h(mu + D Z) >= tau), Z standard Gaussian, no tilt;
- the corrected response, I_1(tau) = E[1{h(mu + D Z) >= tau} w(Z)] with the signed weight w(Z) = 1 + (k . Z)(3 - |Z|^2), k the perspective rate of the shared chart.

The geometry certificate is already in place and is not re-derived here [proved, collaborator]: with W_F = 1 for an indicator, |I_true - I_A| <= 0.7381 |k| and |I_true - I_1| <= 1.8921 |k|^2, the latter conditional on the corrected integral being evaluated exactly. This page is about evaluating I_A and I_1 with a certificate, not about the geometry.

## 3. Moments, exactly, with a price

For m >= 1 the m-th intensity moment E[h(mu + D Z)^m] is an exact Gaussian expectation of |sum_j beta_j exp(b_j . Y)|^{2m} with Y standard Gaussian after the reference envelope is absorbed to the power 2 m, with its own tilt, normalization W_m and coefficients b_j, beta_j given in GABOR-THRESHOLD-MOMENTS.md; polynomial weights in Z are transformed by Z = -2 m H_m D^T A u + H_m^{1/2} Y and must not be applied untransformed [proved, collaborator]. The moments are not the self-products of a truncated feature vector; those change the source (#271).

Evaluation: a positive Gauss-Hermite rule with q nodes per coordinate, applied to the original exponential sum at q^r nodes with |sum|^2 raised to m, at O(q^r (J + log m)) arithmetic given the rule; error at most A_m e^{Lambda} P(Pois(Lambda) >= q) with C = 2 m max_j ||b_j||, Lambda = C^2 / 2, A_m = W_m (sum_j |beta_j|)^{2m}; a polynomial weight of degree s multiplies the error by the explicit factor K_w(C) and shifts q to q - s; a sufficient q is s + n with n >= max(1, 2 e Lambda, log_2(A_m K_w(C) / epsilon)) [proved, collaborator; the one-dimensional Poisson-tail bound checked here on tabulated rules, bridge #274]. The rule's construction and the floating-point error are separate obligations and enter the moment's error interval. Each moment carries its interval [E h^m - e_m, E h^m + e_m].

Growth in the order [proved, collaborator, #275]: with G = D^T A D, u_j = D^T A d_j, v_j = D^T nu_j and L = max_j (u_j^T G^+ u_j + v_j^T G^+ v_j) (a positive-definite common envelope puts every u_j, v_j in the range of G even for a singular D; the safe simpler bound replaces L by max_j (d_j^T A d_j + nu_j^T A^{-1} nu_j)), the m-th moment's Lambda_m is at most m L, linear in the order; and the single-atom identity W_m(c_0) |beta_j|^{2m} exp(2 m^2 ||Re b_j||^2) = |a_j|^{2m} W_m(c_j) gives A_m <= (sum_j |a_j|)^{2m}, so for the normalized intensity x = h / (sum_j |a_j|)^2 the prefactor is at most one and an unweighted normalized moment to error epsilon needs only q >= max(1, 2 e m L, log_2(1 / epsilon)) nodes per axis: a cost linear in the moment order per axis with no hidden J^{2m} evaluation. The amplitude bound and the normalization refer to the original authored atoms, never to tilted or Hermite coefficients. A broad spread of centres and frequencies still makes L large, and q^r is the dimension cost. Open: how the binomial coefficients' growth with n enters the total cost of a degree-n threshold; the coefficient precision looks exponential in n while the node count is logarithmic in that precision, which may keep the ideal arithmetic price polynomial in n; this is distinct from the final threshold error, which needs the actual certified gap.

## 4. The envelope

With x = h / W in [0, 1], a = tau / W, 0 < delta < min(a, 1 - a), epsilon = exp(-2 n delta^2) and S_b(x) = sum_{j >= n b} binom(n, j) x^j (1 - x)^{n - j}:

p^-(x) = S_{a + delta}(x) - epsilon <= 1{x >= a} <= S_{a - delta}(x) + epsilon = p^+(x),

polynomials of degree n in x, hence in h [proved, collaborator; checked here on a grid]. The gap Delta = p^+ - p^- = 2 epsilon + sum of the Bernstein terms between n(a - delta) and n(a + delta) is a nonnegative polynomial, at most 1 + 2 epsilon everywhere and at most 3 epsilon outside |h - tau| <= 2 W delta. Its degree is n, so E Delta and E Delta^2 are combinations of the moments up to order n and 2 n.

Limitation, not to be argued around: p^-(tau) <= 0 and p^+(tau) >= 1 for any continuous global envelopes, so the gap is at least one at the threshold and E Delta is at least the footprint's mass at h = tau. The gap is the band mass computed rather than assumed. A material whose footprint sits on its threshold reports a large gap, which is the correct answer.

## 5. The oracle and its certificate

Affine target: the interval [E p^-(h), E p^+(h)], each endpoint a combination of the moments with the moments' error intervals propagated through the polynomial's coefficients (the coefficients of S_b in the monomial basis alternate in sign and grow with n, so the propagation is by absolute values and is part of the reported error); the point estimate E q(h) with q = (p^+ + p^-) / 2; the certified error E Delta / 2 plus the propagated moment error.

Corrected target: the point estimate E[w q(h)], evaluated as moment queries with the transformed weight; the certified error E[|w| Delta(h)] / 2, bounded by either sqrt((1 + 9 |k|^2) E Delta(h)^2) / 2 (E w^2 = 1 + 9 |k|^2 in two dimensions, checked here) or E[(1 + w^2) Delta(h)] / 4, which adds degree six in Z [proved, collaborator]; multiplying the order relation by the signed weight is not allowed.

Total error reported for the corrected oracle: the geometry term 1.8921 |k|^2 plus the sandwich's certified error plus the propagated moment intervals plus the visibility tail if the footprint reaches the chart's boundary. A display budget is met when the sum is below it; nothing is admitted on the geometry term alone.

## 5b. The band certificate from moments, and the degree law

The gap of section 4 is a band mass in disguise, and the question of which degree n a pixel error needs is the question of how much footprint mass sits near the threshold. Two things are precise about it and one is open.

The certificate [elementary; the distinction from the Christoffel function per #279]. For any polynomial p and any band B = [tau - b, tau + b] on which p(t)^2 >= a > 0 is certified, P(h in B) <= E[p(h)^2] / a. The Christoffel function lambda_n(tau) = min_{p(tau) = 1, deg p <= n} E p(h)^2 bounds the point mass P(h = tau) only; a polynomial normalized at one point need not majorize a band, and its zeros can make the bound useless. The optimal band probability given moments is a generalized moment problem with a globally feasible band-majorizing polynomial (Bertsimas and Popescu 2005, Huerlimann 2015; the Chebyshev-Markov-Stieltjes construction under its canonical-quadrature assumptions), and an ordinary floating-point semidefinite solution is not its own certificate: feasibility must be certified separately, for instance by an exact sum-of-squares decomposition of p^2 - a on B.

The density statement [collaborator, #279]. If ess sup_{|t - tau| <= b} f_h(t) <= M, then P(|h - tau| <= b) <= 2 b M, with b = 2 W delta for the envelope of section 4. A gradient floor on the single level tau is not enough for this; the density must be controlled on the whole band. Critical values need not make the density diverge: a nondegenerate extremum in two dimensions has a finite one-sided density and a saddle a logarithmic singularity, so a critical level worsens the required degree without forcing an infinite one for every tolerance in an atom-free law.

The degree law in the two forms it can take. With the Bernstein envelope, ε = exp(-2 n delta^2), the band half-width is 2 W delta and E Delta <= 4 W delta M + 3 ε, so a gap budget eta is met with delta = eta / (8 W M) and n = 32 W^2 M^2 ln(6 / eta) / eta^2: quadratic in W M / eta, because a Bernstein envelope resolves a step only to width W / sqrt(n). An envelope of the Jackson or Chebyshev kind resolves to width W / n and would make the degree linear in W M / eta; its sandwich property then needs its own certificate (a sum-of-squares identity on [0, W]), which the Bernstein construction gets for free from positivity. [checked here, toy law] On the uniform law on [0, 1] with tau = 0.4 and the constructive polynomial p_m(t) = (1 - (t - tau)^2)^m, a = (1 - b^2)^m, the certificate E p_m^2 / a reads 0.389, 0.200, 0.106 and 0.064 at m = 10, 40, 160, 640 against a true band mass 0.040 at b = 0.02, tracking the resolution 1 / sqrt(2 m) rather than b, which is the sqrt(n) limit in numbers.

Conditioning, an obligation and not a footnote. [checked here] In the monomial basis the absolute coefficient sum of p_m^2 is 1.5e6 at m = 10 and 2.0e24 at m = 40 on that toy, so moments carried as numbers in the monomial basis lose everything long before a useful degree; the collaborator's 3^n bound for the Bernstein envelope is the same growth. The obligation is open [#285]. The proved true-moment rule has a tilt, atom coefficients and nodes that depend on the moment's order m, so a linear combination of the moment-specific estimates is not the same functional as evaluating the envelope at one common set of nodes of h; de Casteljau evaluates a Bernstein polynomial stably once valid node values and weights exist, but it does not connect a common-node estimate to the moment-specific Poisson certificates. Either a common-rule error bound for q(h(Y)) under the untilted Gaussian, or a proved equivalent positive-node construction, is needed before the conditioning obligation can be called met; the collaborator is checking alternatives. A candidate under audit (#302): one maximum-order tilt H_N = (I + 2 N G)^{-1} with reference centre c_0 = mu gives every moment m <= N the same nodes, with importance weights det(I + 2 N G)^{-1/2} w_GH exp(N q); after diagonalizing G the lower moments carry a positive quadratic exponential and a complex linear one whose positive Taylor majorant has an explicit generating-function tail, and degree-graded coefficient errors appear to give a single q = O(N log(N / t)) at fixed spread and envelope scale, the raw weights having mass at most one and normalizing positively; the square completion is checked, and the cutoff constants passed independent audit (#304): for a normalized authored sum, G = O diag(g_i) O^T, gamma = max g_i, c_0 = mu and L as in section 3, the common nodes are Z_a = O diag(1 + 2 N g_i)^{-1/2} y_a with raw weights pi_a = det(I + 2 N G)^{-1/2} w_a exp[N sum_i g_i y_{ai}^2 / (1 + 2 N g_i)] of mass M in (0, 1] (the exact Gaussian mass is one by the moment generating function, and a positive rule with nonnegative deficits gives at most one; checked here per axis with a six-node rule: 0.985, 0.891 and 0.858 at (N, g) = (4, 0.3), (16, 0.3), (16, 1.2)), normalized by M at a cost at most ||p||_inf (1 - M) for an unsigned bounded polynomial since positive weights give |Q p| <= ||p||_inf M; for every m <= N the raw moment error is at most 2^{r/2} e^{4 m L} s_m^{-q} with s_m = 1 + theta_m, theta_m = min(1, (1 + 2 m gamma) / (4 (N - m) gamma)) and s_N = 2, the mass error being the case m = 0; for a degree-N polynomial in shifted Chebyshev form with coefficient absolute sum C and 0 < epsilon <= C, a sufficient common per-axis node count is q >= ceil[16 N L + 8 N ln(2 e N) + 2 max(1, 4 N gamma) ln(2^{r/2 + 1} C (N + 1) / epsilon)], Bernstein coefficients bounded by B using 4 N ln(2 e N) and B instead, the graded bound |c_m| <= C (2 e N / m)^{2m} being what spares the low moments the highest precision; any polynomial bounded by B has C <= (2 N + 1) B, so one positive rule covers the bounded degree-N class; the evaluation is the original field once per node then Clenshaw, ideal work O(q^r [J + N]) with prepared rules, q = O(N log(N / epsilon)) at fixed geometry, numerical rule generation, scaled weights and source rounding extra. An appendix extending raw weighted moments to fixed original-Z polynomials, all latent dimensions preserved, is being finalized; the committed notes are to follow, and the obligation is met conditionally on them. It is a new common-rule proof and not a justification of the earlier common-node shortcut. An exact counterexample (#288): one centred atom f(z) = exp(-a z^2 / 2), x = exp(-a z^2); every m-specific tilted rule computes E x^m = (1 + 2 m a)^{-1/2} exactly with one node, while a one-node common rule under the original Gaussian evaluates p(x) as p(1), wrong already for p = x; a positive cell quadrature with exact original-Gaussian masses has a Lipschitz certificate of its own, at a worse cost. The analytic 3^n bound addresses truncation only and neither erases float precision nor settles the change of quadrature.

The band certificate in its general form [collaborator, #281, independently audited]. Let K be a set containing the support of h (here [0, W]) and B the band. If a polynomial q satisfies q >= 0 on K and q >= 1 on B, both certified, then P(h in B) <= E q(h) = sum_j c_j m_j in the coefficients c_j of q and the moments m_j, and with the moments known to intervals of half-width epsilon_j the bound is sum_j c_j m_hat_j + sum_j |c_j| epsilon_j. If only q >= -eta on K and q >= 1 - eta on B are certified, then q + eta is feasible and the bound gains eta. The form P(B) <= E p^2 / a above is the case q = p^2 / a, whose nonnegativity on K is automatic and whose band bound is the one obligation. Feasibility must be certified, coefficient residuals included; a sum-of-squares identity is one way, with its positive-semidefinite allowances accounted, and an ordinary floating-point solver output is not a certificate.

Two exact counterexamples to keep [collaborator, #280; checked here]. On the uniform law on [-1, 1] the degree-two Christoffel function at the centre is lambda_2(0) = 4 / 9, while P(|X| <= 1 / 2) = 1 / 2: the centre value is not even an upper bound for that band, which is why a band needs a majorant and not a point-normalized minimizer. Inside the Gabor family itself, the single atom F(z) = exp(-|z|^2 / 4) under the standard Gaussian in two dimensions has intensity h = exp(-|Z|^2 / 2) exactly uniform on (0, 1), with density one at its nondegenerate critical maximum: critical levels need classification, not a universal divergence claim.

A constructive majorant with a certificate by construction [derived here; probe paper/tools/exp/theory-probes/jackson-majorant.mjs]. Map the range [0, W] to theta in [0, pi] by t = W (1 - cos theta) / 2, so that a trigonometric polynomial of degree n in theta is an algebraic polynomial of degree n in t. Let J_n(theta) = (3 / (2 pi n (2 n^2 + 1))) (sin(n theta / 2) / sin(theta / 2))^4, the Jackson kernel, nonnegative, even, of unit mass on [-pi, pi], a trigonometric polynomial of degree 2 n - 2; its mass outside [-w, w] is at most delta_n(w) = pi^3 / (n (2 n^2 + 1) w^3), from sin(theta / 2) >= theta / pi on [0, pi] (checked: the bound exceeds the measured tail by a factor near ten at w = 8 / n to 32 / n). For the band B in theta and its widening B' by w on each side, q = 1_{B'} * J_n + delta_n(w) is a trigonometric polynomial with q >= 0 everywhere and q >= 1 on B, both by construction: no search and no sum-of-squares solve, the only obligation being the kernel's tail bound. Its expectation is a combination of the Chebyshev moments E T_k(1 - 2 h / W), k <= 2 n - 2, or is evaluated at the quadrature nodes directly. [checked here, toy laws] On the uniform law with tau = 0.4 and a half-width of 0.02 (true mass 0.040), the certificate reads 0.404, 0.140, 0.072 and 0.055 at n = 32, 128, 512 and 2048 with w = 12 / n; for a half-width of 0.05 (true 0.100), 0.132 at n = 512 and 0.115 at 2048; on a Beta(2, 5) law with tau = 0.4 and half-width 0.02 (true 0.062), 0.107 at n = 512 and 0.080 at 2048; the minimum of q on the band is 1.002 to 1.029 and on the support it equals delta, as constructed. The rate first written here for this construction, an excess like 1 / n, is withdrawn (#288): with w = c / n the tail allowance delta_n = pi^3 n^2 / ((2 n^2 + 1) c^3) tends to pi^3 / (2 c^3), a nonzero floor (0.0090 at c = 12), which the sweep had not reached; balancing the widening against the cubic tail gives n^{-3/4} at best. The Jackson construction is kept only as the simplest certificate by construction.

The Selberg majorant [Selberg; Vaaler 1985; Montgomery, Ten Lectures, chapter 1; built and checked here in paper/tools/exp/theory-probes/beurling-selberg.mjs]. Beurling's function B(z) = (sin(pi z) / pi)^2 [sum_{n >= 0} (z - n)^{-2} - sum_{n >= 1} (z + n)^{-2} + 2 / z] satisfies B(x) >= sgn(x) with integral of B - sgn equal to one and a Fourier transform supported in [-1, 1]; checked: B - sgn has minimum 0 on a grid of [-20, 20] (attained at the integers, where B(m) = sgn(m) by the removable singularity), and its integral over [-60, 60] is 0.9983 with a tail of order 0.0017 beyond. For an interval [alpha, beta] of the circle of length one and a degree N, F(x) = (B(delta (x - alpha)) + B(delta (beta - x))) / 2 with delta = N + 1 majorizes the indicator with excess 1 / delta, and its periodization is a trigonometric polynomial of degree N: checked, the periodized majorant of [0.3, 0.35] has mean 0.08029 against beta - alpha + 1 / (N + 1) = 0.08030 at N = 32 and 0.05775 against 0.05775 at N = 128, its Fourier coefficients fall from 2.6e-4 at k = N to 3.3e-6 at N + 1 and 1.3e-8 at N + 2, and S - chi is at least 2.5e-4 and 2.5e-5 on the grid. The certificate P(h in B) <= E S(theta(h) / (2 pi)) then holds by construction. Its excess is exactly 1 / (N + 1) against the uniform measure of the circle; against a law with density f in x = theta / (2 pi) it is at most (sup f) / (N + 1) when f is bounded, an upper bound and not an equality (#292), and the supremum need not be finite: within the coherent-mixture class itself the single atom h = exp(-a |Z|^2) with a > 1 has f_h(t) = (1 / (2 a)) t^{1 / (2 a) - 1}, unbounded as t -> 0, and in the circle variable a density like x^{1 / a - 1}, unbounded near zero, so the coherent-mixture theorem's local density bound near a positive threshold does not give a global supremum after the circle transform; a localized-density-plus-tail bound (the excess function S - chi integrated against f near the band, plus the skirt of S - chi times the far mass), a different variable with priced queries, or a narrower class is needed before (sup f) / (N + 1) is a source-degree law. [checked here, toy laws] Uniform law, tau 0.4, half-width 0.02 (true 0.040): 0.131, 0.064, 0.046 at N = 32, 128, 512, against the Jackson construction's 0.140 and 0.072 at 128 and 512; the measured excesses 0.0237 at 128 and 0.0060 at 512 equal the law's density 3.078 over 129 and 513, 0.0239 and 0.0060. Half-width 0.05 (true 0.100): 0.190, 0.124, 0.106. Tau 0.1, half-width 0.02 (true 0.040): 0.096, 0.054, 0.044. Beta(2, 5), tau 0.4, half-width 0.02 (true 0.062): 0.201, 0.099, 0.072 against Jackson's 0.213 and 0.107; half-width 0.05 (true 0.156): 0.292, 0.192, 0.165; tau 0.1, half-width 0.02 (true 0.078): 0.186, 0.107, 0.086. On the toy laws, whose densities are bounded, the measured excesses 0.0237 and 0.0060 at N = 128 and 512 coincide with 3.078 / (N + 1) because the uniform-in-t law's density in x is nearly constant across the band's neighbourhood; that is the upper bound being nearly attained, not a law. The degree for an excess eta on a law with bounded density is at most (sup f) / eta: tens to low hundreds on these toys, against hundreds to thousands for Jackson. Conversion to intensity moments (#292): the integer moments of h reach only functions of cos(2 pi x) = 1 - 2 h / W, so the envelope must be even in x; a band strictly inside (0, W) pulls back to two reflected arcs of the circle, and the even majorant is the sum of the two arcs' Selberg majorants, with uniform excess 2 / (N + 1) against the circle measure and each cos(k theta) converted through the Chebyshev polynomial T_k(1 - 2 h / W); a one-sided threshold h >= tau pulls back to one arc symmetric about zero and keeps the single-interval excess. The majorant built in the probe is of one arc and is a general trigonometric polynomial, not an intensity polynomial, so its certificates above are checks of the construction and not yet of the moment-based oracle; the even version is the one to build. The remaining obligations: the periodization and series truncations as numerical error (the coefficients beyond N are not exactly zero in the probe), the localized excess bound, and the expectation through the open quadrature obligation below. For the localized excess bound the skirt of the majorant is governed by the decay of B - sgn: [measured, paper/tools/exp/theory-probes/beurling-decay.mjs] between the integers (B - sgn)(x) times (pi x)^2 rises to one from below on the positive side (0.87 at x = 2.5, 0.99 at 40.5) and falls to one from above on the negative side (1.13 at 2.5, 1.01 at 40.5), and the maximum of (B - sgn) x^2 over [1, 60] is 0.101 on the positive side and 0.122 on the negative side, against 1 / pi^2 = 0.101; a proved skirt constant, for instance (B - sgn)(x) <= c / x^2 for |x| >= 1 with c near 0.13, is an obligation the primary-source review may settle, and the localized bound would then read excess <= (sup of f over the band widened by m / (N + 1)) / (N + 1) plus c (N + 1)^{-2} times the integral of f over the far region weighted by the inverse squared distance to the band, each term explicit.

A source-dependent degree law for coherent mixtures [proved, collaborator, #289; independently confirmed]. For positive phase-aligned amplitudes with a common carrier, h = e^{-q} (sum_j beta_j exp(v_j . Z))^2 with beta_j > 0, G = D^T A D and v_j = D^T A (c_j - c_0), the Hessian of -log h is 2 G - 2 Cov_softmax(v); if the whitened rates G^{-1/2} v_j fit in a ball of radius R < 1 then every covariance is at most R^2 G and the Hessian is at least 2 B with B = (1 - R^2) G. In two dimensions a shell of -log h of log-width Delta then has area at most pi Delta / sqrt(det B), and Gaussian rearrangement gives P(|h - tau| <= b) <= 1 - exp(-log((tau + b) / (tau - b)) / (2 sqrt(det B))) for 0 < b < tau, without locating the minimum; a source-space ball containing A^{1/2} c_j of radius R < 1 certifies it for every full-rank D, verified in O(J). The Bernstein degree then follows: with a = tau / W, a desired unweighted gap rho and d_B = sqrt(det B), take delta = min(a / 4, (1 - a) / 2, rho a d_B / 8) and n >= ln(6 / rho) / (2 delta^2), so that the band b = 2 W delta is at most tau / 2 with probability at most rho / 2 and 3 exp(-2 n delta^2) <= rho / 2, giving E Delta <= rho before the moment error. A source-parameter-to-degree link for this subclass, not a uniform cost. Its exact boundary: two positive unit-width atoms at +-a e_1 give h = exp(-x^2 - y^2 - a^2) cosh^2(a x); at a = 1, -log h = 1 + y^2 + x^4 / 6 + O(x^6) and the mass near the maximum scales as b^{3/4}, at a > 1 the origin is a saddle with b log(1 / b) behaviour: positivity alone is not enough, the geometry of the log-intensity decides the law. For general oscillatory atoms the collaborator holds a finite-order directional derivative certificate and analytic Remez bounds (Brudnyi 1999) with non-explicit constants, as separate levels.

An exact structural elimination [proved, collaborator, #290; independently checked]. If all real and imaginary relative atom-rate vectors lie in one real line of the whitened screen coordinates, rotate Z = (u, v) so that relative centres and phases vary only with u; the common envelope gives h(u, v) = A(u) exp(-a (v + d(u))^2) with a = G_vv > 0, d(u) = (G_uv u + l_v) / a and A(u) = h(u, -d(u)) evaluated from the J atoms on the ridge, and for tau > 0 the conditional threshold probability given u is zero when A(u) < tau and Phi(r) - Phi(l) with s = sqrt(log(A / tau) / a), l = -d - s, r = -d + s otherwise, so the two-dimensional threshold mean reduces exactly to a one-dimensional Gaussian integral at O(J) source work per u, no moments and no atom pairs; the cubic signed correction closes in the same coordinates through the truncated Gaussian moments M_j = int_l^r v^j phi, E_v[w H | u] = [1 + k_u u (3 - u^2)] M_0 + k_v (3 - u^2) M_1 - k_u u M_2 - k_v M_3 with M_1 = phi(l) - phi(r) and M_j = l^{j-1} phi(l) - r^{j-1} phi(r) + (j - 1) M_{j-2}; a = 0 handled directly with the conditional weight 1 + k_u u (2 - u^2). An elimination rule, not a general fast integral: a crossing A(u) = tau makes a square-root cusp in the outer response, relative frequency can make arbitrarily many crossings, and the outer integral needs its own certificate; but it avoids a high-degree polynomial price where one coordinate integrates the threshold exactly. Structural elimination and material approximation are to be compared at the theory level before one moment representation is chosen.

The envelope in its final form [collaborator, #296 to #298, primary sources Akiyama and Tanigawa 2004 and Morgenbesser; GABOR-LEVEL-GEOMETRY.md section 4a at e75c0b5; checked here]. For the one-sided threshold h >= tau, with n = N + 1, a = arcsin(sqrt(tau / W)) / pi and v_k = pi (k / n)(1 - k / n) cot(pi k / n) + k / n, the finite algebraic bracket is

p_N^{+-}(t) = 1 - 2 a +- 1 / n + sum_{k = 1}^N [ -2 v_k sin(2 pi k a) / (pi k) +- (2 / n)(1 - k / n) cos(2 pi k a) ] T_k(1 - 2 t / W),

the even Selberg bracket of the arc [a, 1 - a] of the circle, from V_N(x) = -sum_k v_k sin(2 pi k x) / (pi k) and the Fejer kernel K_N(x) = (1 / n)[sin(pi n x) / sin(pi x)]^2 = 1 + 2 sum_k (1 - k / n) cos(2 pi k x) of unit circle mass, with the reflected V terms giving the sine coefficients and the two Fejer terms the cosine ones. It has N finite coefficients, no Beurling series tail and no periodization; its uniform error from a certified absolute coefficient error epsilon_c is at most epsilon_c since |T_k| <= 1, and feasibility is kept by adding epsilon_c to the majorant and subtracting it from the minorant. Its width is exact, p^+ - p^- = [K_N(x - a) + K_N(x - 1 + a)] / n, and each one-sided uniform excess against the circle measure is 1 / n. The localized bound, without any global density [collaborator, #296]. If the symmetric lifted density rho on the circle is at most M within circle distance d of the 2 m endpoints of m arcs, each one-sided expected error is at most m M / n + 2 m / (n^2 sin^2(pi d)), and the full bracket width has the direct bound 2 m M / n + 2 m / (n^2 sin^2(pi d)), the far term not doubled: near the endpoints the exact integrated excess or width is used, away from them K_N / n <= 1 / (n^2 sin^2(pi d)) and the remaining probability is at most one. ## 6. What a probe must report

For one synthetic field with J, A, the centres, frequencies and amplitudes stated, one footprint (mu, D, k) stated, one threshold tau: the moments to order 2 n with their intervals and the q used for each; the envelope's n and delta and epsilon; the interval [E p^-, E p^+], the point estimate, the certified error, and the corrected estimate with its error; the true response by a Monte Carlo with its own noise, for comparison only; and the arithmetic count (nodes times atoms per moment, summed). The result is a diagnostic of the contract on that field, not a cost claim.

## 7. What this page does not cover

Unequal atom widths, periodized atom fields, hashed noise, normalized normals and BRDFs, general material graphs, and any small uniform cost. The geometry certificate covers every bounded material on the shared chart; the material integration covered here is one family, and its price is source-dependent.
